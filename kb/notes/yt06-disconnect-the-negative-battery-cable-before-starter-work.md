The first step in any starter replacement is to **disconnect the negative battery cable**. The
starter's heavy feed cable is always live from the battery, so a tool slipping against ground
while that terminal is connected can cause a dead short, arcing, or burns. Removing the negative
cable de-energizes the whole circuit and makes the rest of the job safe.

This is standard practice for the `electrical-starting` system, but be aware of the side effects
on a 1994 F-150: disconnecting the battery also clears the PCM's adaptive (learned) strategy, so
the engine may idle or run slightly differently for several miles after reconnecting while it
relearns. On a truck with an air bag, the manual also calls for disarming the air bag and waiting
before disturbing battery, column, or wheel components.
