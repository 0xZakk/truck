On the 1994 F-150's rear drums, the shoes slide on raised contact pads on the backing plate. The FSM warns that grooved contact pads may cause the rear brakes to drag or stick. The right fix is to clean the pads, lightly sand off burrs, and coat them with high-temperature silicone lubricant before reinstalling the shoes — but if the pads are actually grooved, the backing plate should be replaced.

The warning is explicit about the wrong fix: grinding or filing the grooves out makes the new shoes/linings sit at the wrong height relative to the drum, which can produce grabby or chirping brakes. Separately, a loose backing-plate (check the mounting bolts) makes the rear brakes very sensitive or grabby. So in the `brakes` system, a dragging, grabbing, or chirping rear brake should send you to the backing-plate condition and mounting, not just the shoes or adjuster.

> "Grooved contact pads may cause the rear brakes to drag or stick. If you attempt to remove the grooves by grinding or filing, the new shoes/linings will not line up correctly with the drum. This may result in grabby or chirping brakes."
