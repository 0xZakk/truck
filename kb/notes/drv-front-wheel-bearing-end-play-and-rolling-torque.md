After the F-150 front wheel bearing is adjusted, the FSM gives two measurable values that
confirm it is right:

- **Bearing end play: 0.006-0.127 mm (0.00025-0.005 in)** — a small amount of axial free play,
  not preload.
- **Torque required to rotate the hub: 1.13-2.82 N·m (10-25 in·lb).**

These give an objective check beyond "feel" and bracket the acceptable range from "too loose"
to "too tight": a dial indicator on the hub face confirms the end play, and a torque
wrench/spring scale on a wheel stud confirms rolling torque. Zero end play (preload) means the
bearing is too tight and will overheat; excessive end play means it is too loose and will
wobble, knock, and wear the rotor/races. The companion adjusting-nut figures are 23-34 N·m
(17-25 ft·lb) to seat, then a final snug of 2.03-2.26 N·m (18-20 in·lb). This spec supports the
`rear-axle` and `suspension` inventory systems whenever servicing front hubs.

> "End play should be 0.006-0.127mm (.00025-.005 in). Torque required to rotate the hub should be
> 1.13-2.82 Nm (10-25 in lb)."
