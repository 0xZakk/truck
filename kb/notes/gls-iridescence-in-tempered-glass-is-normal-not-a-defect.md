A customer may report grayish or bluish, fairly geometric iridescent spots ("mottling") in the
side or rear windows, often more visible under certain lighting or through polarized sunglasses.
Per TSB 93-21-1 this is a normal characteristic of tempered glass, not a defect. It is caused by
the polarizing properties the tempering process imparts via internal tension and compression —
the very forces that make tempered glass strong — interacting with polarized natural light.

The fix is to explain the condition, not replace the glass: mottling does not affect glass
quality or durability, cannot be reduced by scouring the surface, and replacement is "neither
warranted nor necessarily effective." Windshields, being annealed rather than tempered, never
show this. A useful triage point for `glass` / `body-cab` complaints — don't chase a warranty
swap for an inherent property.
