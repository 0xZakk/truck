The 1994 F-150 wiper motor uses permanent magnets made of ceramic. The FSM opens its
test procedure with a caution that these magnets can shatter or crack if the motor
receives a severe physical shock, and that improper handling can damage the magnets
and make the motor inoperative.

Practically, this means a wiper motor that died after being dropped, struck during
cowl work, or banged during shipping may be mechanically intact but magnetically dead
— it will fail the current-draw test or simply not turn. Handle the motor gently
during removal and installation (`electrical-body`), and treat a sudden no-run failure
after impact as a likely cracked-magnet motor rather than a wiring problem.
