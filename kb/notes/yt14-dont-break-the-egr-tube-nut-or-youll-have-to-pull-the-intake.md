The single biggest risk in this EGR job is shearing off the large EGR tube nut (the
1-1/16 inch fitting at the bottom of the valve). If it breaks, the repair escalates
dramatically: you then have to remove the intake manifold to replace the EGR tube itself,
which on this `engine`/intake setup means disconnecting the many wires and vacuum hoses
that crowd the plenum.

The mitigation is simple and cheap — drench the nut and every fastener in penetrating fluid
before applying force, and back it out slowly, working it loose rather than muscling it.
The host credits the penetrant for letting the nut and the plenum bolts come free cleanly
without needing a backup open-end wrench. On a truck this age, seized emissions hardware is
the norm, so patience here saves a much larger teardown.
