On a 25-plus-year-old truck the starter's hardware is the failure point, not the job
itself. Because he skipped penetrating fluid, the host **stripped the lower starter mounting
bolt** and **snapped a brittle electrical connector** during reassembly. The fix is
preventive: **spray the mounting bolts and cable studs with penetrating oil and let them
soak** before turning anything, which lowers the chance of rounding or shearing aged
fasteners in the `electrical-starting` system on a 1994 F-150.

Before bolting on the new starter, **wire-brush the mounting surface** to clear dirt, rust,
and debris. The starter case grounds through that mounting flange, so a clean seat helps
both the mechanical fit and the ground path. If an electrical connector does break, it can
be replaced/re-terminated rather than scrapping the harness, but careful handling avoids
the problem entirely.
