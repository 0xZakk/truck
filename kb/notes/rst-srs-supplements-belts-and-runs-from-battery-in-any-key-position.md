The 1994 F-150's Supplemental Air Bag Restraint System is exactly that — supplemental. It adds frontal
crash protection for front-seat occupants *on top of* the three-point belts, and Ford is explicit that belt
use is still required to get the full benefit. The system is built from an electrical system, the
driver-side air bag module, the primary (impact) sensors, a safing sensor, the air bag diagnostic monitor,
the air bag indicator, and a tone generator.

The key operational fact to internalize is that the SRS is powered directly from the battery, not switched
through the ignition. It can fire with the key in any position, including OFF and LOCK, and even when the
front seat is unoccupied. That is why air bag service is dangerous any time the battery is connected, and
why disarming is a battery-disconnect-plus-wait procedure rather than just turning the key off. The
electrical system does three things: detect impacts, supply power to the igniter(s), and monitor readiness.
This system lives in the `interior` and `body-cab` inventory areas.

> "The supplemental air bag restraint system can function with the ignition switch in any position,
> including OFF and LOCK. The supplemental air bag restraint system can also function when the front seat
> is unoccupied."
