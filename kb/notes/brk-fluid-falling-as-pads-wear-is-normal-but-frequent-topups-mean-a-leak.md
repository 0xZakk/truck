The FSM lists several master-cylinder behaviours that are normal and should not be mistaken for faults. The reservoir level rises during brake application and falls during release, with no net change after a full apply-and-release cycle. A trace of brake fluid on the booster shell just below the master cylinder mounting flange is normal — it comes from the bore-and-seal lubricating action. And the fluid level gradually decreases as the disc pads wear, because the caliper pistons move out and hold more fluid in the calipers.

The diagnostic line is drawn at how often you add fluid. The FSM's fluid-level-check guidance is blunt: "If brake fluid has to be added often, check all hydraulic connections for leaks." So a slow drop tracking pad wear is expected in the `brakes` system, but a reservoir that needs repeated topping-up is an external leak to be traced at the connections, hoses, calipers, and wheel cylinders.

> "Condition 3: Fluid level will decrease with pad wear."

> "If brake fluid has to be added often, check all hydraulic connections for leaks."
