Two details make the heater-core swap go well. First, the original foam and rubber **sealing
material** inside the housing cavity tends to have **disintegrated** with age; clean it out and
transfer or replace the foam before fitting the new core so the cover seals against the case
and air is routed through the core instead of leaking past it.

Second, because the heater core is part of the engine `cooling` loop, after the hoses are
reconnected you must **refill and bleed the system** to clear trapped air before checking for
heat. In the video the narrator fills, bleeds "just a bit," then confirms warm air with **no
steam at the vents** — steam or a sweet smell at the vents would indicate the new core or a
connection is leaking. Use the correct Ford 50/50 coolant when topping off rather than plain
water.
