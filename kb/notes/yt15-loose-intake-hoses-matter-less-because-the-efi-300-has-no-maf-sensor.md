The 1994 4.9L EFI 300 is a **speed-density** system: it estimates airflow from manifold
pressure (MAP), RPM, and temperature rather than measuring it directly. Because there is **no
mass-airflow (MAF) sensor** in the intake tract, a slightly loose air intake hose does not
corrupt an airflow measurement the way it would on a MAF-metered engine — unmetered air after a
MAF is what causes lean trim problems on those systems.

That does not mean intake hoses can be ignored: the presenter still reseats them and says they
should "go back like they're supposed to," since a disconnected or badly fitted hose can let in
dirt and disrupt intake routing. The point is only that on this engine a loose hose is a lower
priority than a corroded TPS or IAC connector when chasing hesitation and hard-start
complaints.

This relates to the `fuel` and `engine` systems.
