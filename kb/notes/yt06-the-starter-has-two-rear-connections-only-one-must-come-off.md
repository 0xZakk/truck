At the back of the starter there are **two electrical connections**, and the video distinguishes
between them so you don't disconnect more than necessary. One is a **wire that runs directly into
the starter** body and is left attached — you do not need to touch it to remove the unit. The other
is a **heavy cable on its own stud** that must be unbolted; once it is off, the starter pulls free.

Knowing which is which saves time and avoids disturbing an internal connection unnecessarily. On
reassembly, reconnect the terminal you removed and make sure the heavy cable nut is properly
tightened, since this stud carries the full cranking current for the `electrical-starting` system.
The video does not name the terminals or give a torque value; the factory manual covers the
solenoid terminal identification and the correct nut torque.
