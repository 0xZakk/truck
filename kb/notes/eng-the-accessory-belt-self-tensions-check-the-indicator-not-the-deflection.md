The 4.9L drives its accessories with a single serpentine belt on an automatic spring-loaded
tensioner, so there is no belt tension to set by hand. Instead the tensioner carries wear-indicator
marks: if the indicator sits within its range, tension is correct; if the indicator falls outside the
range, the belt is worn or the wrong length and should be replaced. In other words, an "improper
tension" reading is a belt problem, not a tensioner adjustment.

On the `engine` inventory system this means belt service is a quick visual check plus a replacement,
not a pry-and-tighten job. If a replacement belt still reads out of range, suspect the wrong belt
length or a failing tensioner.

> "This vehicle is equipped with an automatic belt tensioner. Belt adjustments are not required, but
> an incorrect or worn belt may cause improper tension. Check the indicator marks on tensioner. If the
> indicator mark is not within the indicator range, replace the belt."
