The 1994 F-150's rear drum brakes are self-adjusting: driving in reverse and applying the brakes sharply, then driving forward, ratchets the automatic adjuster to take up clearance — repeat several times to fully adjust. Manual adjustment is only needed after the shoes are relined/replaced or the adjuster (lever kit 2A176) has malfunctioned.

To set them by hand (drum installed): with the transmission in neutral and the vehicle safely raised, remove the adjusting-hole cover at the bottom of the backing plate and turn the adjusting screw (part of nut 2047) with Brake Adjusting Tool D81L-1103-C until the shoes drag and lock the drum, then back off the adjusting screw an additional 10 to 12 notches so the drum turns freely without drag. After adjusting both sides, apply the brakes — if the pedal travels more than halfway to the floor there is too much shoe-to-drum clearance, so repeat the expand/back-off steps. Use a vacuum (never compressed air) to remove brake dust, use non-asbestos parts, and also check the parking-brake cable adjustment and free equalizer movement. In the `brakes` system, this manual reset restores a firm pedal after a rear brake job.

> "When the shoes are against the brake drum, loosen the adjusting screw... an additional 10 to 12 notches so that the brake drum rotates freely without drag."
