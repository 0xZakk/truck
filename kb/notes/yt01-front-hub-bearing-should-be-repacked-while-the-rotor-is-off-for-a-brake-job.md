Because pulling the rotor on an OBS F-150 also exposes the front wheel bearing that rides on the
spindle, the video treats a brake job as the natural time to service that bearing: clean the old
dirty grease out of the hub cavity, repack the bearing with fresh grease, and replace the
bearing and seals if anything was leaking oil/grease behind the assembly. A cheap bearing-packer
tool (cited around $20) makes the repack easy.

This is relevant to a stock 2WD 1994 F-150 even though the video is on a 4x4: the 2WD truck has
adjustable tapered-roller front bearings that must come apart to remove the rotor, so they get
repacked and re-set on every pad/rotor job. The 4x4's "snug the inner nut, back off a quarter
turn, confirm free spin" sequence shown here is the same seat-then-back-off idea the factory
manual specifies for the 2WD bearing — but for actual numbers, follow the FSM, which seats the
2WD bearing at 17–25 ft·lb, backs off about a half turn, then re-torques to 18–20 in·lb rather
than going by feel. This is a `brakes`/`suspension` (front-hub) task.
