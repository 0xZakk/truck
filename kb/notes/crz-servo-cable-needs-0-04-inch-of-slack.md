The speed control actuator (Bowden) cable connects the servo to the throttle linkage, and its
adjustment deliberately leaves a small amount of slack. The FSM procedure: remove the cable
attaching clip at the throttle bracket, set the throttle plate in the closed position, pull
the actuator cable to take up any slack, then back off at least one notch so that 0.04 inch of
slack remains. Hold the cable in that position and install the attaching clip.

The reason for the slack is in the FSM's own note: "For proper operation, ensure cable is not
pulled tight." A cable adjusted with no slack holds the throttle slightly open at idle and can
prevent the throttle from closing fully, which on this `electrical-body` system shows up as a
high or hanging idle rather than an obvious cruise complaint. Too much slack, conversely,
makes the servo waste travel before it can act on the throttle.

> "Back off at least one notch ensuring there is 0.04 inch of slack in cable. NOTE: For proper
> operation, ensure cable is not pulled tight."
