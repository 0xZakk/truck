The F-150 carries its rear axle on a pair of semi-elliptic leaf springs — the classic
pickup-truck arrangement that handles heavy bed loads. Each spring is clamped to the axle by
U-bolts, bolts solidly to a front bracket at its forward eye, and pivots through a swinging
rear shackle at its rear eye where it attaches to the frame. The shackle lets the spring
lengthen and shorten as it flattens under load.

Shock absorbers damp the springs (upper and lower eye mounts), and a rear stabilizer bar with
end links limits body roll. This load-carrying simplicity is why the rear suspension is robust
but rides stiffer than the coil-sprung front. Worn shackle bushings, a shifted spring (broken
center pin), or loose U-bolts show up as rear-end wander or a clunk, the common faults to chase
in this part of the `suspension` system.
