The F-150 manual clutch is operated hydraulically rather than by a cable or mechanical linkage.
Pressing the clutch pedal drives the clutch master cylinder, which pushes **DOT 3 brake fluid**
through a quick-connect coupling to a **concentric (internal) slave cylinder**. On this design the
slave is an annular cylinder that surrounds the transmission input shaft inside the bellhousing
and pushes directly on the clutch release bearing — there is no external fork-and-rod to adjust.

Because the slave lives inside the bellhousing and the whole circuit is sealed, the system is
self-adjusting and needs no free-play adjustment, but it also can't be bled by pedal-pumping
alone: the coupling has to be opened to purge air. This is a key part of the `driveline` system,
and it shares its DOT 3 fluid family with the brakes (but is a separate reservoir).

> "Clean area around clutch master cylinder reservoir cap, then remove cap and fill to step with
> suitable DOT 3 brake fluid."
