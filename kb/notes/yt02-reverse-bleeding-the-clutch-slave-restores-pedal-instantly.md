When a new clutch slave cylinder leaves you with no pedal and conventional top-down
bleeding (pump the pedal, crack the bleeder, repeat) won't purge the air, a **reverse
bleed** gets the pedal back immediately. Instead of trying to pull or push air *down*
out of the system, you force fresh fluid *up* from the slave's bleed valve to the
clutch master cylinder, sweeping the trapped air out the top where it naturally wants
to rise.

Procedure as shown: fill a large syringe with **DOT 3 brake fluid** and force a short
length of small-bore tubing (the narrator uses "fish hose") over the tip. Press the
tubing onto the slave cylinder's bleed valve, open the valve, and push fluid through
until it flows out of the clutch master cylinder reservoir at the top. Close the
bleeder, top off the reservoir, and the pedal should be firm right away. Keep an eye on
the reservoir so it doesn't overflow as fluid arrives.

This works because the F-150's hydraulic clutch is a sealed `driveline` circuit with a
concentric (internal) slave, so air pockets are easy to trap and hard to dislodge by
pedal-pumping alone; moving fluid from the low point to the high point is the reliable
direction. DOT 3 is the correct fluid for this circuit per the FSM. Note this is a
field technique, not a factory-documented method — capture the displaced old fluid and
verify the reservoir is filled to the step when finished.
