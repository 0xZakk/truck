When the driver reports a visible shake in the floor pan, seats, steering column, or
front-end sheet metal that appears at highway speed — 72 km/h (45 mph) and up — the FSM
Road Test Diagnosis table points to a rotating wheel-end fault: wheel, tire, axle, or
brake imbalance or run-out. This is the classic speed-dependent shake that gets worse as
speed climbs and is felt rather than just heard.

Confirm it is wheel-end before balancing: per the same table, if the shake disappears
while coasting in neutral it is NOT a wheel-end component, so the neutral-coast test
should be run first. If it survives neutral coast, inspect tires for run-out and uneven
wear, check wheel and tire balance, and look at brake-rotor/drum run-out and axle run-out.
On the 1994 F-150 (`engine`), a single out-of-balance tire or a bent wheel is the most
common cause of this complaint.

> "Visible shake in floor pan, seats, steering column and/or front end sheet metal at
> highway speed, 72 km/h (45 mph) and up. — Wheel/tire/axle/brake imbalance or run-out."
