The air bag in the steering wheel hub must stay electrically connected while the wheel turns, and the air bag
sliding contact (clockspring / spiral cable) is what makes that possible. Mounted on the steering column
behind the wheel, it continuously transfers the SRS electrical signals between the column wire harness and
the contact in the steering wheel, maintaining the deployment-circuit connection through full steering
travel without a hard wire that would twist and break.

This is why the clockspring is a common SRS fault point and why it must be handled and centered carefully
during column or wheel service — a broken or mis-timed sliding contact can open the air bag circuit and set a
fault. It rides on the same energy-absorbing steering column (two breakaway capsules and a bendstrap for
controlled collapse) at the `interior`/steering boundary.

> harness to the contact in the steering wheel for the supplemental air bag restraint system..."
