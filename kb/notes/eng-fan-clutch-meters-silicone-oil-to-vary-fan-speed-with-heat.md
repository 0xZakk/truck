The 4.9L's engine-driven fan uses a thermal viscous (fan drive) clutch — a fluid coupling
filled with silicone oil. Fan speed is set by how much torque the silicone oil can carry: more
oil in the coupling drives the fan faster, less oil lets it slip and turn slower. A
heat-sensitive coiled bi-metallic spring connected to an opening plate meters the oil, so as
underhood/radiator temperature rises the clutch engages harder and the fan speeds up, and as
things cool it backs off.

This variable engagement is why the fan "roars" briefly on a hot start and then quiets down: at
cruise with adequate airflow the clutch slips to save power and noise, only locking up when the
`cooling` inventory system actually needs maximum airflow.

> "The fan drive clutch is a fluid coupling containing silicone oil. ... The more silicone oil
> in the coupling, the greater the fan speed, and the less silicone oil, the slower the fan speed."
