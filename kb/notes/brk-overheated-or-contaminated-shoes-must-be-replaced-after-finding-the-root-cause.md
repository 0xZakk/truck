When inspecting rear shoes on the 1994 F-150, the FSM flags two replace-and-investigate conditions. Overheating shows as a glazed (blackened, shiny) lining, bluish-purple discoloration of the shoe webbing, or warping (the shoe wobbles when laid flat on a hard surface). Contamination shows as brake fluid on the lining (leaking wheel cylinder) or axle grease (leaking rear axle seal). Either condition means the shoes are replaced.

Crucially, the FSM says to find the cause before replacing — overheated shoes point to a dragging/sticking problem, and grease contamination "is often the result of worn axle bearings" whose excess lateral play has damaged the axle seal. So in the `brakes` system, contaminated rear shoes can be a symptom of a failing rear axle bearing, not just a bad seal; replacing the shoes without fixing the leak or drag source just repeats the failure.

> "Leakage from the rear axle seals is often the result of worn axle bearings. As the bearings deteriorate they develop excessive lateral play, which in turn damages the axle seals."
