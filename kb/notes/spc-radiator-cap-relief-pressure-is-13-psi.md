The cooling-system pressure cap on the 1994 F-150 has a **relief pressure of 13 psi**. By
holding the sealed system at up to 13 psi above atmospheric, the cap raises the boiling
point of the 50/50 coolant so the engine (inventory id `engine`) can run hot without
boiling over, and it seats a vacuum valve that lets coolant return from the recovery bottle
on cool-down.

A cap that relieves early (weak spring or damaged seal) lets the system boil and push
coolant out at normal operating temperature; one that fails to vacuum-relieve can collapse
a hose on cool-down. Testing the cap to its 13 psi rating with a cooling-system pressure
tester is part of diagnosing overheating or coolant-loss complaints.
