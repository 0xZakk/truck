To adjust base timing you rotate the distributor, which requires loosening its hold-down bolt at
the base. Back the bolt off only a little — just enough that the distributor will turn under hand
pressure but still has enough friction to hold whatever position you leave it in. There is not
much clamping torque to begin with, so a small amount of loosening is plenty; don't free it up so
much that it flops around.

The reason for the light touch is the sequence at the end of the adjustment: with the engine
running you rotate the distributor to the desired mark, but to tighten the bolt you must shut the
engine off, and you don't want the distributor to drift while your hand is off it. Leaving a bit
of hold lets it stay put through the shutdown so the setting doesn't change before you torque the
bolt back down, then you re-check the mark on the `engine`. This complements the SPOUT-disconnect
step of the timing procedure.
