The 4.9L secondary ignition cables conduct coil high voltage to each plug in firing order and are
built with a carbon-impregnated, multi-filament synthetic fiber core inside heat-resistant rubber
insulation. That carbon core gives the cables built-in internal resistance, which suppresses radio
static and eliminates interference.

The practical takeaway for the `engine`: measurable resistance in these wires is normal and
intended, not a defect. When testing, you are looking for an open or wildly out-of-range cable, not
expecting near-zero ohms like a solid conductor. The rubber insulation is engineered for underhood
heat, so cracked or hardened insulation is a real failure mode that lets spark leak to ground.
