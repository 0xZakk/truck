The customer's 4.9L F-150 started fine when cold but, after the engine warmed up — the owner
ran into a store and came back to a hot engine — it would not crank. With a known-good
battery, clean connections and clean grounds, and a working charging system, that pattern
points to a **failing starter** rather than the battery or cables. This is the classic
**heat-soak** symptom in the `electrical-starting` system: as the starter (and the engine bay
around it) heats up, marginal brushes, windings, or a weakening solenoid that still works cold
can no longer pull enough current to spin the motor.

The diagnostic discipline matters as much as the symptom. Before condemning the starter, the
host confirms the battery is good, the cable connections and grounds are clean, and the
alternator is charging — only then is a hot no-crank attributable to the starter. On a high-mileage
truck (this one had ~270k miles) an original or tired starter that drags or clicks only when hot
is a reasonable replacement candidate once those upstream checks pass.

Don't confuse this with a heat-soaked starter caused by a missing or melted heat shield, or with
voltage-drop problems in aged battery cables — both can mimic a "bad starter." Verify the supply
side (battery, cables, grounds) under load first; if those are solid and the no-crank tracks
engine temperature, the starter is the culprit.
