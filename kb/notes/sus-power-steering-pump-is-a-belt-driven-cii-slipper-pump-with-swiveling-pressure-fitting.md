The F-150's hydraulic supply comes from the Ford CII power steering pump — a belt-driven
slipper-type pump with its reservoir built right onto it. The reservoir is fiberglass-
reinforced nylon and bolts to the pump's front plate, so the pump body sits inside the
reservoir housing. The high-pressure hose connects with a quick-connect fitting below the
filler neck on the outboard side of the reservoir.

One frequently misread detail: that pressure-hose fitting is designed to swivel. A line that
rotates at the fitting is normal and does NOT mean the connection is loose, so it should not
be "tightened" in response. Internally the slipper pump develops the flow and pressure that
the XR-50 gear uses for assist; pump health is verified by flow and relief-pressure specs and
the system pressure test. This is the source of all hydraulic effort in the `steering` system.
