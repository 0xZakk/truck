The door-pillar switch that turns on the 1994 F-150's interior courtesy and dome lamps is a
plunger switch held to the body by a hex sleeve nut and held to the main wiring by a
twist-lock with three tabs spaced 120 degrees apart. The same FSM procedure covers both the
"interior light switch" and the "dome lamp switch."

The design tolerates wear: if the engaged tab breaks off when you disconnect the switch from
the wiring, you rotate the switch 120 degrees to engage a fresh tab instead of replacing the
part — only once all three tabs are broken must the switch be replaced. To install, twist
the wiring and switch 4-1/2 turns counterclockwise, push the wiring back through the pillar
hole, then screw the switch clockwise into the pillar until seated and confirm the lamp
works by opening and closing the door. This is part of the truck's `electrical-body` system.

> "If all three tabs have been broken, the courtesy lamp switch must be replaced."
