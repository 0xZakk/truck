Deployment is a four-step chain, and the FSM notes steps 1-3 all happen in a fraction of a second. First,
the sensors detect a severe frontal impact and the required combination closes, sending current to the
inflator to ignite the gas generant chemicals. Second, those chemicals burn rapidly inside the metal
inflator, producing nitrogen gas plus small amounts of dust, both of which are cooled and filtered during
inflation. Third, the inflating bag splits the molded tear seams in the steering-wheel trim cover and
unfolds in front of the driver. Fourth, after inflation the gas escapes through vent holes in the bag and
it deflates very rapidly.

Understanding this sequence explains several service realities: the bag is a one-shot pyrotechnic device,
the "smoke" after a deployment is filtered nitrogen and inert dust rather than a fire, and a deployed bag
is found already deflated. The whole event centers on the steering-wheel module in the `interior` area.

> "The inflating air bag splits open the trim cover. The air bag then rapidly unfolds and inflates in front
> of the driver."
