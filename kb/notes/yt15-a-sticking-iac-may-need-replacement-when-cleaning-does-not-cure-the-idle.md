Cleaning the idle air control (IAC) connector can restore a good connection, but it does not
fix a mechanically worn or sticking valve. In this video the IAC still sticks after the
connector is cleaned, and with the truck at about 203,000 miles the presenter concludes it
"probably actually needs a new one." The takeaway is that connector cleaning and valve
replacement address different failure modes: a corroded connector is an electrical problem, a
sticking valve is a worn/dirty mechanical problem.

If idle remains rough, hangs, or surges after cleaning, the IAC valve itself is the next
suspect. On the EEC-IV system a PCM that cannot hold target idle through the IAC sets idle
control codes (e.g. DTC 411/412), so pulling codes helps confirm whether the IAC is at fault.

This relates to the `engine` system and EEC-IV idle control.
