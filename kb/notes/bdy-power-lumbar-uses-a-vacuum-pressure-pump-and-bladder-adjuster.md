The 1994 F-150's optional adjustable lumbar support is a pneumatic system, not a mechanical
crank. A seat-back pad adjuster — an inflatable bladder — is retained by bend-over tabs on the
seat-back frame and is fed by an adjusting hose. An electrically driven vacuum/pressure pump
inflates or deflates that bladder through the hose, changing the firmness/contour of the
seat back.

Because the bladder lives inside the seat-back behind the cover and the pump lives down in the
seat cushion, servicing either means dismantling the seat: the pump is reached by removing the
seat, the seat tracks, and the seat-cushion cover, then disconnecting its adjusting hose and
electrical connector, while the bladder is reached by removing the seat-back cover and bending
its retaining tabs. Knowing it is a sealed air system explains common symptoms — a lumbar that
won't hold or won't inflate usually means a leaking hose/bladder or a dead pump, both in the
`body-cab` seat system.
