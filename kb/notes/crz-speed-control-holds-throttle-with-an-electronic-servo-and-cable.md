The 1994 F-150's cruise control is an electronic-servo system, not a vacuum-only system. The
servo/amplifier assembly — a throttle actuator with integrated electronics — sits in the
left-hand rear corner of the engine compartment and is tied to the throttle linkage with an
actuator (Bowden) cable. To hold a set speed, the amplifier compares the truck's actual speed
to the set speed and commands the servo to pull or release the cable, adding or subtracting
throttle.

Around this actuator are the supporting parts that make it usable and safe: the steering-wheel
driver control switches (On/Off, Set-Acc, Coast, Resume), the amplifier, the horn relay, and
the deactivation inputs (brake lamp switch, brake pressure switch, and on manual trucks the
clutch switch). Understanding that the servo is the muscle and the switches are the nervous
system is the key to diagnosing this part of the `electrical-body` system: a complaint is
either "the servo isn't moving the throttle" or "an input is telling it to stay off."

> "The system is comprised of an electronic servo which consists of a throttle actuator with
> integrated electronics ... connected to the throttle linkage with an actuator cable."
