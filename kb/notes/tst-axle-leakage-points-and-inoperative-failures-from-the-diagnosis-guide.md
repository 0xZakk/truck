Beyond noise and vibration, the FSM Diagnosis Guide enumerates two further categories.
For Leakage Conditions it lists the drive-pinion seal, drive-pinion nut, axle-cover
gasket, axle-shaft seals, casting porosity (holes in the casting), weld leaks, and the
axle vent and/or hose — so a wet axle gets traced to a specific seal, gasket, or housing
defect rather than guessed at. A clogged or missing axle vent, notably, can pressurize the
housing and push lube past otherwise-good seals.

For Inoperative Conditions — outright failures — it lists a broken axle shaft, broken
pinion stem, broken welds, wheel bearing, axle lock-up, broken end yoke, broken u-joint,
and broken gear teeth. These are the catastrophic faults that leave the truck immobile or
unsafe. On the 1994 F-150 (`engine`), use this category to recognize when a noise or
sudden loss of drive is a structural failure requiring teardown rather than an
adjustment-level fix.

> "Leakage Conditions — Drive Pinion Seal, Drive Pinion Nut, Axle Cover Gasket, Axle Shaft
> Seals, Casting Porosity, Weld Leaks, Axle Vent and/or Hose. Inoperative Conditions —
> Broken Axle Shaft, Broken Pinion Stem, Broken Welds, Wheel Bearing, Axle Lock-Up, Broken
> End Yoke, Broken U-Joint, Broken Gear Teeth."
