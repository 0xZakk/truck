Wheel lug torque on the 1994 F-150 is 135 Nm (100 lb ft) for the 5-lug 1/2-20 wheel and 190 Nm
(140 lb ft) for the 8-lug 9/16-18 wheel. Ford specifies these for clean, dirt- and paint-free, dry
threads and explicitly says never to use oil or grease on the studs and nuts.

The dry-thread requirement is important: a torque spec assumes a given friction coefficient, and
lubricating the threads lets the nut turn farther for the same torque, over-stretching the stud and
risking failure or a warped rotor. On the `brakes`/wheel end, correct and even lug torque also keeps
the rotor from being clamped distorted, which would show up as pedal pulsation.
