The Ford 300's character comes from its geometry: a **long stroke** combined with a
**relatively low compression ratio** (~8:1–9:1). That combination trades top-end horsepower
for **strong torque low in the rev range — peak torque around 1,600–2,200 RPM**. The engine
is happiest pulling from just off idle, which is exactly what a work truck needs for towing
and hauling.

This is why the 300 is a "workhorse, not a racehorse." Enthusiasts who chase horsepower
eventually hit the design's ceiling — the video's narrator is even swapping his 300 for a V8
for that reason. But for a stock 1994 F-150 meant to haul and last, the low-RPM torque and
the simple, durable construction are the whole point.

> "The 300-six is known for its ability to produce high torque at low RPMs, with peak torque
> often occurring around 1,600 to 2,200 RPM."
