On the OBS F-150 the front rotor is not a one-piece part — it is held to the hub by the **wheel
studs**, which are pressed through both. To reuse a hub but resurface the rotor, the studs are
hammered out from the front (they drop out the back). A sacrificial lug nut is threaded a few
turns onto each stud before hammering so the blows land on the nut, not the bare threads, which
keeps the stud end from mushrooming; if a stud is damaged it can be replaced. If the rotor is
seized to the hub flange, an air hammer worked around the lip breaks the rust seal so the two
parts separate.

The payoff is economic: with the rotor split from the hub you can have just the rotor turned
(cited at roughly $10–15) rather than buying a complete hub/rotor assembly. On reassembly the
mating surface can be wire-wheeled and given a light coat of anti-seize so it comes apart easier
next time, then the studs are re-driven (an air hammer with a snub tip works well). Small
remaining gaps are acceptable because **torquing the lug nuts pulls the rotor fully home against
the hub**. This is a `brakes`-system bench procedure; note that whether the rotor is worth
turning still depends on the FSM minimum-thickness limit, so measure before resurfacing.
