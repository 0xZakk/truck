On the EFI 4.9L, the gasket between the throttle body and the intake manifold can deteriorate
or get **sucked inward**, opening a vacuum leak that shows up as a rough or unstable idle.
Ford issued a technical service bulletin about exactly this gasket. The deteriorated rubber
vacuum lines that connect to the throttle body fail in the same way and add to the leak.

A characteristic clue: the engine **idles rough but accelerates fine**, and the roughness is
felt in the seat even though the engine looks smooth. Recent tune-up parts (plugs, wires,
cap, rotor, filters) being good helps point away from ignition and toward the air/vacuum
path. The fix in the field was to pull and clean the throttle body, replace the failed TB
gasket, and replace the soft, deteriorated vacuum lines.

> "There was a bulletin about the gasket being sucked in and causing a vacuum leak… Looks
> like the TB gasket might have failed."

Note one limitation of the carb-cleaner spray test here: spraying around the hoses and TB
produced **no** idle change, which would normally argue against a vacuum leak — yet the leak
was real, at the gasket face. Absence of a spray response doesn't fully rule a leak out.

This relates to truck inventory systems `fuel` and `intake-exhaust`.
