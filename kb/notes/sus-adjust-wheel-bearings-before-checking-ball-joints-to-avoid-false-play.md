A loose front wheel bearing and a worn ball joint produce the same symptom at the tire: rock
the wheel and it moves in and out. That is why the FSM's very first step in the ball-joint
inspection is "adjust front wheel bearings." If you skip it, slack in the bearing shows up as
movement when you rock the tire and you can wrongly condemn a good ball joint (or, worse,
mask a bad one).

The practical diagnostic order for a "front-end is loose" complaint on this truck is
therefore: set the wheel bearing preload first, then run the 1/32-inch rock test on the upper
and lower ball joints. Only play that remains after the bearings are correctly adjusted
counts against the joints. This sequencing keeps `suspension` diagnosis honest.
