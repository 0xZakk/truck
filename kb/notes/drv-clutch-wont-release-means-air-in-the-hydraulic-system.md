On the F-150's hydraulic clutch, the FSM's own diagnostic tell is direct: if the clutch does not
release fully after a hydraulic-system service, that indicates **air has remained in the system**,
and the fix is to repeat the bleed procedure. Symptoms of incomplete release include the truck
being hard to get into gear (especially first and reverse from a stop), gear clash on shifts, and
the truck creeping with the pedal fully down.

Air is the prime suspect because the concentric slave is sealed inside the bellhousing and the
reservoir must be kept full during any service — letting the master cylinder run dry, or not
following the timed pedal-cycling steps, leaves air trapped that pedal pressure can't fully
compress. This belongs to the `driveline` system, and the cure is a correct re-bleed rather than
clutch component replacement.

> "If clutch does not release fully, indicating air has remained in system, repeat procedure."
