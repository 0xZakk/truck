The FSM Diagnosis Guide treats a clunk — the sharp take-up noise heard when shifting from
drive to reverse or when getting on and off the throttle — as a total-driveline lash
problem rather than a single part. Its listed causes are total axle backlash, worn
driveshaft u-joints, loose or broken engine mounts, driveshaft splines needing lube, worn
driveshaft slip-yoke splines, axle-shaft spline fit, and an inoperative throttle damper.

Because the noise is the cumulative free play in the powertrain taking up under load, the
diagnosis is to find and add up the slack: check axle backlash, inspect u-joints for wear,
verify engine/transmission mounts are tight, and lube the slip-yoke and driveshaft splines.
On the 1994 F-150 (`engine`), a dry slip yoke or worn u-joints are common, cheap-to-fix
contributors to a driveline clunk before any axle disassembly is warranted.

> "Clunk Noise — Total Axle Backlash, Worn Driveshaft U-joints, Loose or Broken Engine
> Mounts, Driveshaft splines need lube, Worn Driveshaft Slip Yoke Splines, Axle Shaft
> Spline Fit, Inoperative Throttle Damper."
