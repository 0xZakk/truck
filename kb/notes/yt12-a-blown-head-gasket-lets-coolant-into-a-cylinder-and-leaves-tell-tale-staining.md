When a 4.9L (300 cu in) head gasket fails between a coolant passage and an adjacent
combustion chamber, coolant gets pulled into that cylinder. In this teardown the owner's
engine ran with the breach before being pulled, and the damage was localized to a single
cylinder — the rest looked normal. On the `engine`, the leak path is gasket-only here; the
bore itself was undamaged.

The diagnostic tells on teardown are distinctive. The affected cylinder's combustion chamber
is **discolored** — essentially rust staining from the antifreeze and water that sat in it —
while the other chambers are clean. The piston crown of that cylinder is also unusually
clean, effectively **steam-cleaned** by the coolant burning off, compared to the normal carbon
buildup on the other pistons. Laying the old gasket on the bench and finding the burned-through
spot confirms the failure point.

For someone diagnosing a stock truck, this is what coolant-into-cylinder failure looks like
from the inside, and it complements the external symptoms (white exhaust smoke, dropping
coolant level with no visible leak, fouling on one plug). It ties into the `cooling` system
because the loss path is the coolant being consumed through that breached cylinder.
