The Ford XR-50 is an integral recirculating-ball power steering gear. A worm shaft turns a
one-piece rack-piston whose teeth mesh with the sector shaft that swings the pitman arm.
Hydraulic assist is metered by a torsion bar: when the driver turns the wheel, the torsion
bar twists in proportion to steering effort, and that twist displaces a rotary valve that
directs pump pressure to one side or the other of the rack-piston.

Because the piston is double-acting — pressure can be applied to either of its faces — the
same cylinder boosts both left and right turns, and the power cylinder is cast integral with
the gear housing. All the working hydraulic passages run inside the gear between valve and
cylinder, which is why the only external plumbing on the `steering` system is the pressure
and return hose pair to the pump.

The elegance of the torsion-bar/rotary-valve scheme is that assist is naturally proportional:
little effort, little twist, little boost; hard effort against a curb, more twist and more
boost.
