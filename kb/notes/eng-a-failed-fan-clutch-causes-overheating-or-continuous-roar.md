A fan clutch fails in two opposite directions, and the symptom tells you which. If it
free-wheels (slips even when hot), the fan can't pull enough air and the engine overheats; the
FSM confirms this by checking for drag over five hand turns and replacing the clutch if none is
felt. A small fluid leak alone is usually harmless, but an excessive leak that bleeds out the
silicone oil leads to the same overheating.

The other failure is a locked-up clutch. Brief noise on a cold start is normal as the clutch
redistributes settled fluid, but a continuous roar or excessive engine-driven fan noise means
the assembly has seized internally — confirmed if the fan won't rotate by hand or feels rough and
abrasive. Separately, some lateral wobble at the fan tip is normal (up to 1/4 in / 6.5 mm) and is
not a reason to replace the clutch on the `cooling` inventory system.

> "Continuous fan noise or an excessive roar indicates the clutch assembly is locked-up due to
> internal failure. ... If the fan cannot be rotated manually or there is a rough, abrasive feel
> as it is rotated, the clutch should be replaced."
