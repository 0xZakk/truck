The Ford 8.8-inch rear axle in the F-150 sets drive-pinion bearing preload with a **collapsible
spacer** between the two pinion bearings. As the pinion nut is tightened, the spacer crushes
slightly, drawing the bearings together and building rotational drag (preload). The target is a
rotational torque, not a fixed nut torque.

Because the spacer crushes permanently, it is a **one-shot part**: the FSM warns that if the
bearing preload exceeds specification before the pinion nut reaches its torque, the collapsible
spacer must be **replaced** — you cannot back the nut off to reduce preload, since the spacer is
already crushed. The pinion nut figure of 217 ft·lb is therefore an upper reference reached only
as needed to hit preload, not a blind torque target. This is core to servicing the `rear-axle`
inventory system, and getting it wrong shortens pinion bearing life or causes whine.

> "If pinion bearing preload exceeds specification before torque is obtained, replace collapsible spacer."
