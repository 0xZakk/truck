The 1994 F-150 windshield is bonded to the body opening with a urethane adhesive, not seated in
a rubber gasket. That bond is structural: a cured urethane windshield contributes to cab
rigidity and to occupant retention, which is why replacement is treated as a safety repair
rather than trim work. Only urethane may be used to service air or water leaks on
urethane-installed glass.

Because the adhesive carries load, the FSM is explicit that the vehicle must not be driven until
the urethane has cured — driving on an uncured bond can jar the glass out of position and
compromise retention. The procedure also has a limited-shelf-life caveat on the Essex urethane
products, which must be discarded once expired. This is core `glass` / `body-cab` knowledge for
any windshield job.
