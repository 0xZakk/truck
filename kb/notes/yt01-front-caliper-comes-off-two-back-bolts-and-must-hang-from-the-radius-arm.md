On the OBS Ford F-150 the front disc-brake caliper is retained by two bolts on the *back* side
of the caliper — one near the top and one near the bottom, with 1/2-inch heads in the video.
With those bolts out, the caliper is freed by working a short pry bar inside it to pop the
bottom and then the top off the rotor. The brake pads (and often the pad bracket) come out with
it; new pads typically ship with new brackets, so a bracket that pops loose is not a problem.

The load-bearing caution is to **support the caliper so it never hangs from the rubber brake
hose** — the presenter sets it up on the radius arm while the rotor work is done. Letting the
caliper dangle stretches or kinks the flexible hose and can damage it internally, so it is hung
or laid aside every time. This is the everyday access step for any front pad or rotor service in
the `brakes` system; on this truck the caliper is a pin/bolt-mounted single-piston unit that
slides off the rotor once those two bolts are out.
