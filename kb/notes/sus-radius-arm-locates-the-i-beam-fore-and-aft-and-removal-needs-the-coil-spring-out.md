On the Twin I-Beam front end, the I-beam axle pivots at its inboard end but would swing
freely fore-and-aft without something to locate it. That job belongs to the radius arm: it
runs back from the outboard end of the beam to a frame bracket, where it is anchored through
rubber insulators, washers, and a spacer. The radius arm controls braking and acceleration
loads (caster-keeping) while also serving as the lower seat for the front coil spring.

Because the coil spring seats on the radius arm, the spring must come out before the arm
can be removed. The FSM order is: remove the coil spring, remove the spring lower seat from
the radius arm, remove the radius-arm-to-axle nut and bolt, then remove the rear washer,
insulator, and spacer from the radius arm rear bracket before pulling the arm. Worn radius-arm
bushings show up as caster wander and clunking, which matters for the `suspension` system.
