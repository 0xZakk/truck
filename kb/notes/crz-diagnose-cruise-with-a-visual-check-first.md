Before any pinpoint testing, the FSM requires a visual inspection to catch obvious faults:
check for bare, broken, or disconnected wires, and verify the servo throttle actuator function
and free operation of the throttle linkage. This front-loads the cheap, common failures —
wiring, connectors, and a binding or maladjusted throttle linkage — before time is spent on
circuit testing.

There is also a hard prerequisite: if the amber check anti-lock brake (ABS) indicator stays
lit with the ignition in Run, service the rear anti-lock brakes before continuing speed
control diagnosis. Because cruise shares the brake and ABS inputs on the `electrical-body`
system, an unresolved ABS fault can corrupt the cruise diagnosis and send you chasing the
wrong circuit.

> "If the amber check anti-lock brake indicator remains lit when the ignition switch is in the
> Run position, service the rear anti-lock brakes before continuing the speed control
> diagnosis."
