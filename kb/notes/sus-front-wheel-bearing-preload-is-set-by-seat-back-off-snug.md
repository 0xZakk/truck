The 2WD F-150 front tapered-roller bearings are not torqued to a single value — they are set
by a three-move sequence that seats the rollers and then leaves a tiny, controlled preload.
First, with the rotor spinning, tighten the adjusting nut to 23-34 Nm (17-25 ft lb) to fully
seat the bearings and races. Second, back the nut off about one half turn to release that
seating load. Third, while still rotating the rotor, snug the nut to just 2.03-2.26 Nm
(18-20 IN lb).

The high seating torque guarantees the bearing is fully home; backing off and re-snugging to
inch-pounds leaves the slight running clearance a tapered bearing needs so it neither binds
nor knocks. Before adjusting, rock the hub to push the disc pads back for running clearance —
but never pry on the caliper's phenolic piston. Finish with a NEW cotter pin, both ends bent
around the retainer. This procedure underpins front-end feel and ties into the `suspension`
system.
