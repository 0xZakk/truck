The FSM's operational test for the fan clutch is tactile. First inspect the fan for cracks or
separations (a damaged fan must never be spun up). Run the engine at a fast idle of about
1500 rpm until it reaches normal operating temperature — blocking the radiator with cardboard
speeds this up — and in any case run it at least five minutes immediately before testing. Then
shut the engine off and, protecting your hand with a glove or cloth, feel the effort needed to
turn the fan by hand: considerable drag means the coupling is working, while spinning with very
little effort means the clutch has failed and should be replaced.

The clutch can also be confirmed on the road: disconnect the bi-metallic spring and rotate it
90 degrees counterclockwise, which disables the temperature-controlled free-wheeling and makes
the clutch behave like a conventional always-on fan. If that cures an overheating condition on
the `cooling` inventory system, the clutch is the culprit and should be replaced.

> "If considerable effort is required, it can be assumed that the coupling is operating
> satisfactorily. If very little effort is required to turn the fan, the coupling is not
> operating properly and should be replaced."
