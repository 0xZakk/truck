On the Twin I-Beam front end, alignment angles change with ride height, so a reading is
meaningless if the truck sits crooked. Before checking caster and camber, confirm front ride
height is within 0 - 3/8 inch side-to-side; if it is off, redistribute or slightly load the
vehicle, and if it still cannot be brought in, verify correct spring installation. Also
equalize tire pressures and confirm front tires match in size, ply, and load range, and that
the front wheel bearings are properly adjusted.

There is a second trap: alignment equipment only reads true when the frame is horizontal. If
the frame is tilted (from tire, spring, or load differences), the caster reading must be
corrected — front higher than rear, SUBTRACT the frame angle; front lower than rear, ADD it
(measured with a spirit protractor on the lower frame flange near the rear-spring front
hanger). Chasing a caster/camber spec without these checks leads to "adjusting" a truck that
was never out of alignment, a classic `suspension` diagnostic error.
