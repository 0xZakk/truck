Both the rear wheel cylinders and the front calipers are inspected the same way in the 1994 F-150's `brakes` system. The dust boots are checked for brake-fluid leakage; a slight amount of moisture is normal (from the initial assembly lubrication of piston and cylinder), but actual fluid leakage means the unit is overhauled or replaced. The bores are checked for rust, scores, or other damage, and the bleeder screw is verified clean, open, and not frozen before reassembly.

A leaking wheel cylinder is also a common source of the brake-fluid contamination that ruins drum shoes, so a leak found here ties directly to shoe replacement. Mounting is verified too — the wheel cylinder must be firmly attached to the backing plate. Caliper inspection adds checks for cracked housing/piston, torn piston dust boots, and corroded or nicked slide pins and bushings, with replacement or rebuild if any are found.

> "Inspect the dust boots for signs of brake fluid leakage. If any leakage is present the wheel cylinders should be replaced or overhauled. A slight amount of moisture is normal."
