The 4.9L F-150 starter has two electrical studs on its solenoid: a small **S (start-trigger)
terminal** held by a **10 mm nut**, and a larger **battery-cable terminal** held by a **13 mm /
½ in nut**. Before disconnecting either, photograph or note how the wires face — the host
points out the S wire was angled downward — so the new starter's wires can be routed exactly
the same way. The reason is safety, not just neatness: if a wire is allowed to lean against the
exhaust it can melt the insulation and **short, or even start a fire**. This is part of the
`electrical-starting` system and ties into routing near the `engine` exhaust.

When installing the new starter, set its nose into the bell housing first while clearing the
nearby **O2 sensor**, then position the body so the terminals sit the same way the old one did
before tightening the nuts. Keep the **heavy red battery cable** oriented upward/away from the
manifold and snug the small S terminal so the trigger wire can't vibrate loose or sag onto hot
parts.

The two mounting bolts also matter to the wiring: the **lower bolt captures a ground wire** under
its head, so thread the new lower bolt through that ground eyelet and start it by hand before
running it down. Tighten the starter so it seats flush against the bell housing and backing
plate. The video gives no torque figures for the stud nuts or mounting bolts — use the factory
service manual values, since a loose battery-cable nut will overheat under cranking current.
