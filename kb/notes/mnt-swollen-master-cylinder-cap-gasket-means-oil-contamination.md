If brake fluid is contaminated with a hydrocarbon/mineral-based fluid — power steering or
transmission fluid accidentally added to the master cylinder — it can be detected by an obvious
swelling of the brake master cylinder filler-cap gasket. That swelling is the diagnostic tell: the
petroleum fluid attacks and expands the rubber.

When this swollen-gasket condition is present, the fix is not a simple fluid swap. All brake-system
rubber parts must be replaced, and all brake tubes thoroughly flushed with Ford High Performance
DOT 3 (C6AZ-19542-AA) or a DOT 3 equivalent (Ford spec ESA-M6C25-A) before the vehicle returns to
service. The reasoning: the contaminant has already begun swelling every seal, cup, and hose in the
`brakes` hydraulics, so partial cleanup leaves degraded rubber that will fail.
