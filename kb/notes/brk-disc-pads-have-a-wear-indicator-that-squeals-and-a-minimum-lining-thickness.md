The front disc pads on the 1994 F-150 have a built-in wear indicator that makes a metallic squeal when the lining has worn to the point where replacement is required — an audible warning before metal-to-metal contact. On inspection the lining thickness should be at least 1/8 in (3 mm) for riveted pads and 1/16 in (1.5 mm) for non-riveted pads, measured to catch the thinnest spot.

The pads are also checked for damage and flatness: linings must not be cracked or glazed (a shiny, glass- or charcoal-like appearance from overheating), and when the inboard and outboard lining surfaces are held together the gap at the middle must not exceed 0.13 mm (0.005 in), for new or used pads. Within the `brakes` system, the squeal is the everyday cue to inspect; these thickness and flatness numbers decide whether the pads pass or get replaced.

> "The brake lining thickness should be at least 1/8 in (3mm) for riveted pads and 1/16 in (1.5mm) for non-riveted pads."
