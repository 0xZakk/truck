The 1994 F-150 FSM front alignment specs, as optimum-plus-range values, are: camber optimum
0.25° with a ±0.5° range; total toe optimum 0.06° with a ±0.25° range; caster minimum 2.0° and
maximum 6.0°. Camber split is optimum 0.0° (±0.4°) and caster split optimum -0.5° (±0.4°),
describing the allowable left-to-right difference. The positive camber and modest toe-in are
typical for a Twin I-Beam truck, and the split figures limit how different the left and right
wheels may be, which is what keeps the truck from pulling.

These numbers ground tire-wear and steering-pull diagnosis and serve as the pass/fail targets
when aligning the suspension. Positive camber outside range or a toe error scrubs tires and pulls
the truck; caster affects straight-line stability and steering return. They are only valid once
ride height and frame level have been verified. Because the front bearings and steering geometry
share the same wheel end, alignment is typically checked alongside bearing preload and tire
condition when the front end is serviced.
