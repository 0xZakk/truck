R-134a in the 1994 F-150 system (inventory id `hvac`) is a non-corrosive, non-explosive,
non-flammable hydrofluorocarbon — but the FSM stresses two real hazards. First, although
non-flammable on its own, it becomes combustible when mixed with high concentrations of air at
higher pressures, so it must never be mixed with air for leak testing or pressurized with shop
air. Second, liquid R-134a evaporates instantly at atmospheric pressure and will freeze
anything it touches; skin or eye contact requires flushing with water for 15 minutes and a
doctor.

Because R-134a is always under pressure, never apply heat to any part of the system, and never
heat a container above 125°F — keep refrigerant away from flames, glowing metal, torches, steam
cleaning, and welding to avoid an explosion.
