The 1994 F-150's 4.9L (300 cu in) inline-six is a fuel-injected engine with a 4.00 x 3.98 in
bore and stroke and an 8.8:1 compression ratio. Rated output depends on the rear axle ratio:
most trucks make 150 hp @ 3400 rpm and 260 ft-lb @ 2000 rpm, while trucks with the tall 2.73
or 3.08 axle are rated slightly differently at 145 hp @ 3400 and 265 ft-lb @ 2000.

The low compression ratio and a torque peak at just 2000 rpm explain the 300's reputation as a
low-revving, torque-first work engine — useful context when judging whether the truck is
"running right" rather than chasing peak numbers. These figures back the `engine` inventory
system.
