The FSM Road Test Diagnosis table describes a telltale signature for engine-driven
vibration: it disappears in neutral-coast drive mode but reappears during a standstill
engine run-up at a specific rpm. Because the complaint tracks engine speed rather than
road speed, the table attributes it to an engine accessory drive or mount that is not
properly adjusted or tightened, not running true, or an accessory that is malfunctioning.

The standstill run-up is the confirming test: park the truck, hold the engine at the rpm
where the customer feels the vibration, and see whether it reproduces. If it does, the
fault is upstream of the wheels. On the 1994 F-150's 4.9L inline-six (`engine`), suspect
items include an out-of-balance or seized accessory pulley, a loose accessory bracket, or
worn engine mounts that let the engine resonate. This complements the neutral-coast test,
which first rules out the rotating wheel-end components.

> "Engine-rpm-related vibration or noise that disappears in neutral-coast drive mode, and
> appears in standstill engine run-up at specific rpm. — Engine accessory drive or mount
> not properly adjusted or tightened, not running true, or accessory malfunction."
