On a 1992–1996 (OBS) Ford F-150 the heater core lives in a plastic case behind and below the
glove box, and it can be serviced from inside the cab without a full dash removal. Let the
engine cool first, then on the engine-bay side disconnect the **two heater hoses** at the
firewall — these feed coolant from the `cooling` system into the core, so plan for residual
spillage and keep a towel under the case.

Inside the cab, remove a small electronics module that hangs in front of the case (held by
two fasteners) and the heater-case cover, which is retained by **seven bolts**. With the
cover off, the core drops down out of the case and pulls free. Reassembly is the reverse:
feed the new core in, fit the **new foam seal** supplied with it, reinstall the cover and
module, and reconnect the heater hoses last (from outside) so the core does not shift while
the cover is tightened. This is an `hvac` repair that also touches `cooling` because the core
is plumbed into the engine coolant loop.

All the case fasteners use an **8 mm or 5⁄16"** head.
