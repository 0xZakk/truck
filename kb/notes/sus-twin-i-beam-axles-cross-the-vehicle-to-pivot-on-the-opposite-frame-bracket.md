The 2WD 1994 F-150 carries Ford's Twin I-Beam independent front suspension: two separate
forged I-beam axles, one per front wheel. What makes the geometry distinctive is that each
beam is long and crosses the vehicle — its outboard end holds the wheel (spindle, ball
joints, radius arm) while its inboard end reaches across and pivots on a frame bracket
mounted on the *opposite* side. The two beams therefore overlap like crossed swords under
the engine.

This crossing layout is the whole point of the design: a long beam means a wheel can travel
up and down through a big arc while changing camber only slightly, giving an independent
front end far more durable than a short-arm setup but with more camber change than a true
A-arm suspension. It also explains why ride-height and alignment are coupled on this truck —
the beam pivot location fixes how camber moves with load.

Each spindle bolts to a spindle arm that ties into the steering linkage, so the front-end
geometry, steering, and the `suspension` system all hang off these two beams.
