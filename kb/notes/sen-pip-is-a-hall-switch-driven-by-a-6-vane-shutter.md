The 4.9L's engine-position signal is generated by a Hall-effect switch in the distributor, not a
crankshaft sensor. A 6-bladed shutter plate, driven by the camshaft, rotates through the gap of
the stator pickup. When a vane enters the gap it distorts the magnetic field and turns the Hall
output ON; when the vane leaves, the field returns and the output turns OFF. The result is a
clean square wave that swings between 0.0 and 12.0 V — the Profile Ignition Pickup (PIP) signal.

This one signal carries crankshaft position, base timing, and engine speed (rpm). The Ignition
Control Module (ICM) reads the raw Hall output and converts it into the PIP signal that goes to
the PCM, which uses it both to time the spark (advance and dwell) and to time fuel injection.

For the `ignition` system this is the heartbeat: lose PIP and the engine won't run, because the
PCM has no position reference. A 12 V square wave on a scope, scaling cleanly with cranking and
running speed, is the signature of a healthy sensor.
