The 1994 F-150's parking brake is mechanical and cable-actuated, working through a parking-brake mechanism inside each rear wheel (drum) brake assembly. A release handle is built into the parking brake control (2780). The control assemblies are self-adjusting and, per the FSM, require no adjustment.

Because the parking brake shares the rear drum hardware, its effectiveness depends on the rear shoes and drums being in good condition and properly adjusted. The FSM does note that when servicing the rear drum shoes you should still check the rear parking-brake cable and conduit (2A635) for proper adjustment and verify the equalizer moves freely — so in the `brakes` system, a weak parking brake usually traces back to worn rear shoes, a seized cable, or a stuck equalizer rather than a separate adjuster.

> "Each rear wheel brake assembly contains a cable-actuated parking brake assembly, and use a release handle which is built into the parking brake control (2780). These control assemblies are self-adjusting and require no adjustments."
