Faced with a blown head gasket, the owner weighed swapping in a spare 300 he had on hand
against repairing the engine already in the truck. He rejected the swap: the spare had been
**sitting outside**, had **no oil in it**, and he had **never heard it run** — its condition
was entirely unknown and could have been a waste of effort. The engine in the truck, by
contrast, was a **known-good runner** whose only fault was the blown gasket, so he ordered a
gasket and repaired it.

This is a useful judgment call for any 4.9L `engine` owner. A head-gasket failure sounds
catastrophic, but if the rest of the engine ran well and the bore is undamaged (as it was
here), a gasket job restores a proven engine. An unproven junkyard or barn-find replacement
introduces unknowns — internal corrosion from sitting dry, seized rings, or rust in the
cooling jackets — that can turn a clean repair into a gamble.

The practical takeaway: diagnose before you swap. The cheaper, lower-risk path is often to fix
the known engine rather than gamble on a spare you can't verify.
