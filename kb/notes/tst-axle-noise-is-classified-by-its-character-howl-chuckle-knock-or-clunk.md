The FSM Diagnosis Guide organizes rear-axle internal noise by the sound the customer
hears, because each character maps to a different mechanical fault. Gear howl and whine
calls for inspecting the gear set, checking gear-set backlash and ring-gear run-out, and
checking bearing preload — i.e., a ring-and-pinion setup problem. Chuckle noise points to
differential gear clearance or broken gear teeth. Knock noise points to gear-tooth
mutilation, ring-gear bolts, or axle-shaft end play.

Naming the sound therefore narrows the teardown before it begins: a continuous howl that
varies with vehicle speed is a gear-mesh/setup issue, an intermittent chuckle on coast is
a clearance or chipped-tooth issue, and a sharp knock is impact from a damaged tooth or
excessive end play. On the 1994 F-150 (`engine`) with its 8.8-inch rear axle, this lets a
technician decide whether to check gear pattern and preload or to look for physical tooth
damage and shaft end play.

> "Gear Howl and Whine — Inspect Gear Set, Check Gear Set Backlash, Check Ring Gear
> Run-out, Check Bearing Preload. Chuckle Noise — Differential Gear Clearance, Broken Gear
> Teeth. Knock Noise — Gear Tooth Mutilation, Ring Gear Bolts, Axle Shaft End Play."
