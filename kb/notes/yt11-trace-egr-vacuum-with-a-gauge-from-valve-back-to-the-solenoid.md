To find an EGR vacuum problem on the 4.9L (`engine`), use a hand vacuum gauge and trace the
signal from the valve back toward the source. First pull the vacuum line off the top of the EGR
valve and tee it into the gauge with the engine running; the working signal is very slight, so
watch the needle closely rather than expecting a large swing. Warm the engine first and blip the
throttle a little to make sure you are pulling good manifold vacuum during the test.

If there is no vacuum at the valve, move to the EGR vacuum control solenoid and pull its lines.
On this truck the line going to the valve matched the color of the line at the valve (green),
and the manifold-supply line was the white one. Gauging the white manifold line tells you
whether vacuum is even reaching the solenoid: a dead reading there means the leak is upstream of
the solenoid, so continue tracing the hoses back — in the video, on toward the EVAP canister —
until you find the break.

After repairing the leak, re-verify in the same order: confirm strong manifold vacuum at the
solenoid's supply line, then confirm the slight needle deflection reappears at the EGR valve,
which proves the solenoid is now passing vacuum through to the valve. A clean road test with the
check-engine light staying off confirms the 332 is cleared.
