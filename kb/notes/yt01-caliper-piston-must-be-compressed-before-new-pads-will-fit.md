After the worn pads come out, the caliper piston is left extended where the thin old pads held
it. Fresh pads are thicker, so the caliper will not slide back over the new pads and freshly
turned rotor until the piston is pushed back into its bore. The video does this by placing an old
pad against the piston and squeezing it in with a **C-clamp**, using the discarded pad to spread
the load evenly across the piston face rather than cocking it.

With the piston bottomed, the caliper drops back over the new pads and rotor and the two
mounting bolts are tightened. Because the piston pushes brake fluid back up the line, the final
step is to **pump the brake pedal up to rebuild pressure and check the reservoir fluid level**
before driving, then test-drive. This is a routine `brakes`-system step on every pad change;
forcing fluid back toward the master also makes it worth watching the reservoir so it does not
overflow.
