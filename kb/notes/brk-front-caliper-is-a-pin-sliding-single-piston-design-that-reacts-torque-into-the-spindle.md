The 1994 F-150's front brakes use a pin-sliding (floating) caliper with a single hydraulic piston. The caliper bolts to the front wheel spindle through two slide pins, and a single wheel-cylinder piston in the caliper bore presses the inboard pad against the rotor; the reaction slides the caliper on its pins and pulls the outboard pad in. The inlet is at the bottom of the bore and a bleeder screw above it bleeds trapped air, while a square-section seal in an annular groove seals pressure and rubber boots keep road splash out.

A key design point: the pad ends sit in the spindle abutments (with anti-rattle clips, 2B164, for a low-drag sliding surface), so nearly all brake torque is transmitted directly into the spindle rather than through the caliper housing. The hub and rotor are integrally cast, and a bolted rotor shield protects the bearings and inboard rotor face. Front disc brakes in the `brakes` system are self-adjusting — pad wear is taken up automatically by piston travel.

> "The system is designed so nearly all of the brake torque generated between the front disc brake rotor and brake pads is transmitted directly to the front wheel spindle and not through the caliper housing."
