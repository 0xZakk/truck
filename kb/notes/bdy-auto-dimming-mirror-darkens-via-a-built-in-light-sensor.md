The optional automatic day/night inside rearview mirror on the 1994 F-150 is an electrochromic
mirror: instead of a manual flip tab, it changes its own reflectivity. A light sensor built
into the mirror detects glare (such as headlights from a vehicle behind), and in response the
mirror face shifts from highly reflective to slightly reflective, cutting the glare reaching
the driver's eyes.

How aggressively it dims is tunable. A DISTANCE control on the bottom of the unit sets the
mirror's sensitivity to light — effectively how bright the trailing light must be before the
mirror darkens. For diagnosis this means a mirror that "won't dim" or "stays dark" may simply
have its DISTANCE control misadjusted or a blocked/failed sensor, rather than a wiring fault.
This is a `body-cab` / interior-trim component.
