Two of the three front power-window motor retaining screws sit behind the door inner panel
where you cannot reach them, so the FSM's intended procedure is to drill to them. After
removing the trim panel and watershield and disconnecting the motor wiring, you check inside
the door that no wires lie behind the target spots, then drill two 12.7 mm (1/2 in) holes at
the factory drill dimples that are positioned opposite the two unexposed motor drive retainer
screws. With those plus the existing larger access hole, all three screws come out.

You then push the motor outboard to disengage it from the regulator gear, prop the glass full
UP, and pull the motor and drive from inside the door. On reassembly the motor retaining screws
torque to 5.6-9.6 N-m (50-85 in-lb), and — because you just put two holes in a wet area — you
cover the drilled holes with 25.4 mm (1 in) square pieces of pressure-sensitive waterproof body
tape (D6AZ-19627-A or equivalent) and confirm the door drain holes are open before reinstalling
trim. This is a `body-cab` door procedure.
