The power window motor and drive is a one-piece replacement assembly mounted inside the door,
held by three retaining screws. Two of those three screws sit behind solid door inner-panel
sheet metal with no factory access hole, so the FSM's intended procedure is to drill to them.
After removing the trim panel and watershield and disconnecting the motor wiring, you check
inside the door that no electrical wires lie behind the target spots, then drill two 12.7 mm
(1/2 in) holes at the factory drill dimples that are positioned opposite the two unexposed
motor drive retainer screws. With those plus the existing larger access hole, all three screws
come out.

You then push the motor outboard (toward the outer sheet metal) to disengage it from the
regulator gear, prop the glass full UP, and pull the motor and drive from inside the door. On
reassembly the motor retaining screws torque to 5.6-9.6 N-m (50-85 in-lb), and — because you
just put two holes in a wet area — you cover each drilled hole with a 25.4 mm (1 in) square
piece of pressure-sensitive waterproof body tape (D6AZ-19627-A or equivalent) to keep the door
cavity watertight, and confirm the door drain holes are open before reinstalling trim. This
procedure is part of the truck inventory `glass` / `body-cab` systems.
