The FSM Road Test Diagnosis table builds its logic around a simple in-traffic test:
bring the truck to the speed where the complaint occurs, then shift to neutral and coast.
If a vibration disappears during neutral coast, it is NOT caused by wheel-end (rotating)
components such as wheels, tires, the axle, or brakes. Removing engine torque from the
driveline while the wheels keep spinning is what makes this diagnostic split possible.

This single observation steers the rest of the diagnosis. A vibration that persists in
neutral coast is tied to road speed and rotating mass (tire/wheel/axle imbalance or
run-out). A vibration that vanishes in neutral coast is tied to engine load or engine
speed — point the investigation at engine mounts, the exhaust system, driveline angle, or
engine accessories instead. On the 1994 F-150 (`engine`), running this test first prevents
needlessly road-force-balancing tires when the real culprit is a bound-up mount.

> "If vibration disappears while in neutral coast, then vibration is NOT caused by
> wheel-end components."
