The front brake rotor on the 1994 F-150 has these service limits: minimum allowable thickness 0.96 in; maximum thickness variation (parallelism) 0.0005 in; maximum lateral runout 0.003 in; and a rotor surface finish in the 0-65 range (microinch). A rotor below minimum thickness must be replaced, not machined, because there isn't enough material left to safely dissipate heat.

These numbers explain common disc-brake symptoms in the `brakes` system: excessive parallelism variation (thickness that differs around the rotor) causes pulsing pedal feel, and excessive lateral runout causes the same wobble-driven pulsation. When diagnosing a pulsating pedal or steering shimmy under braking, these are the gauge readings that confirm a warped or unevenly worn rotor.

> "Minimum Allowable Thickness 0.96 ... Maximum Variation (Parallelism) 0.0005 ... Lateral Runout 0.003 ... Rotor Surface Finish 0 To 65"
