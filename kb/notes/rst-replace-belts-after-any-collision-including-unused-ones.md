After any collision, every safety belt assembly in the truck must be inspected — retractors, buckles, the
front buckle support (slide bar) assemblies if fitted, and all attaching hardware. The FSM's rule is to
replace belt assemblies unless a qualified technician confirms they show no damage and operate properly.
Crucially, this includes belt assemblies that were NOT in use during the collision: they too must be
inspected and replaced if any damage or improper operation is found.

There is a related sheet-metal caution: if a new retractor is bolted into a damaged or distorted mounting
area it can warp and fail, so the mounting surface must first be reworked back to its original shape and
structural integrity. And if webbing cannot be pulled from a retractor at all, the front belt is replaced.
This contrasts with the air bag crash sensors, which can be reused if undamaged — belts are held to a
replace-unless-proven-good standard in the `interior`.

> "ALL BELT ASSEMBLIES SHOULD BE REPLACED UNLESS A QUALIFIED TECHNICIAN FINDS THE ASSEMBLIES SHOW NO DAMAGE
> AND OPERATE PROPERLY. BELT ASSEMBLIES NOT IN USE DURING A COLLISION SHOULD ALSO BE INSPECTED..."
