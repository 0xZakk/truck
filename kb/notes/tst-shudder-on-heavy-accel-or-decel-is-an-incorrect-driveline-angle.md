The FSM Road Test Diagnosis table assigns a specific, distinct cause to a shudder or
rumble that occurs on heavy acceleration or deceleration across the 0–72 km/h (0–45 mph)
range: incorrect driveline angle(s). Unlike a steady-speed highway shake, this complaint
is load-dependent — it shows up when the driveshaft is transmitting high torque and the
universal-joint working angles momentarily change.

Because u-joints introduce velocity fluctuation that grows with working angle, an
out-of-spec pinion angle or a sagged/altered ride height produces a torque-sensitive
shudder. On the 1994 F-150 (`engine`), check for anything that changed driveline geometry:
worn or collapsed engine/transmission mounts, rear spring sag, lift or lowering, or a
shifted axle. The separate moaning-noise condition in the same table also calls out a
misaligned center bearing on a two-piece driveshaft as a related geometry fault.

> "Shudder or rumble on heavy acceleration or deceleration, 0-72 km/h (45 mph). —
> Incorrect driveline angle(s)."
