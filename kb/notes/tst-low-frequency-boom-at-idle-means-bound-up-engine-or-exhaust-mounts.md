The FSM Road Test Diagnosis table calls out a distinctive booming complaint: a
low-frequency booming noise, or pressure felt in the ear drums, that occurs at or just
above idle. Its assigned cause is engine or exhaust mounts that are bound up. A bound-up
mount creates a solid metal-to-metal path that lets engine and exhaust vibration transmit
into the cab as a low-frequency pressure boom instead of being isolated by the rubber.

The same table lists a related moaning-noise condition (light accel/decel, 40–104 km/h)
that also implicates engine or exhaust mounts bound up, powertrain components grounding,
or undamped powertrain resonance. On the 1994 F-150 (`engine`), inspect the engine mounts
and exhaust hangers for contact with the frame or body, collapsed/hardened rubber, or a
hanger that has shifted so the exhaust touches the underbody. Freeing the grounded path
typically eliminates the boom.

> "Low frequency booming noise or pressure felt in ear drums, which occurs at or just
> above idle. — Engine or exhaust mounts bound-up."
