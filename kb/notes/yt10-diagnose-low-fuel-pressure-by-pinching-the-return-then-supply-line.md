When key-on/engine-off fuel pressure reads low on these EFI trucks, the host works a fixed
ladder to isolate the cause on the `fuel` system. Start with the cheapest suspect: a clogged
fuel filter restricts flow and drops pressure, so replace the filter and re-test. If pressure
is still low, briefly pinch the **return** line at its rubber section with a clamp — if the
pressure now spikes on the gauge, the fuel pressure regulator is dumping too much and should
be replaced. If pinching the return still won't build pressure, the fuel pump itself is the
likely failure.

To catch **leaky injectors**, turn the ignition off and pinch both the supply (pressure) and
return lines for a short moment. If trapped pressure still bleeds down with both lines blocked,
fuel is escaping past the injectors. Pinch hoses only briefly to avoid damaging them, and only
at the rubber sections.

This same logic underlies the FSM leak-down test: after the pump stops, pressure should hold,
and the hose-pinching technique tells you whether the regulator, pump check valve, or injectors
are the leak path.
