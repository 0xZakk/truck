The front and rear outboard seating positions on the 1994 F-150 use a continuous-loop three-point belt: a
single combined lap-and-shoulder belt with a common sliding tongue and one retractor. In normal use the
retractor lets the webbing move freely in and out so the occupant can lean and reach. It only locks when a
mechanically actuated inertia sensor trips — during hard braking, hard cornering, or an impact of about
8 km/h (5 mph) or more.

A useful diagnostic consequence is that you cannot lock this retractor by simply jerking the belt slowly; the
center lap belts, by contrast, have no retractor at all and are adjusted by hand. The inertia-sensing
behavior is what the FSM's road test verifies, and it is the core of how the `interior` belts protect
occupants without restricting normal movement.

> "The shoulder harness retractor is designed to let the webbing freely move in or out at all times, except
> during vehicle hard braking, hard cornering, or impact of 8 km/h (5 mph) or more, when it is automatically
> locked by a mechanically actuated inertia sensor."
