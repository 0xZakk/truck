Unlike many vehicles where the solenoid is integrated into the starter motor (so a bad
solenoid means replacing the whole starter), the 1987–1996 F-150 mounts its **starter
solenoid (starter relay) separately on the inner fender**. This means the solenoid is a
cheap, easy stand-alone replacement that does not require dropping the starter, which is
relevant to the `electrical-starting` system on a stock 1994 F-150.

The fender solenoid is a high-current relay: large terminals carry battery and starter
cables, while a small push-on **trigger wire (the "elbow" connector)** energizes the coil
when the key is held in START. A common no-crank/click symptom is the solenoid itself
failing rather than the starter, so being able to swap it independently is a useful
diagnostic and repair shortcut.
