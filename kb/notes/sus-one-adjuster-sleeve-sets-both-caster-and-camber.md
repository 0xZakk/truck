Unlike an A-arm suspension where caster and camber are tuned with separate shims or cams, the
Twin I-Beam F-150 uses ONE adjuster sleeve per wheel to set both angles at once. The sleeve
sits at the spindle/ball-joint location, and swapping to a different sleeve rotates the
spindle to move caster and camber together.

Adjusters come in 1/2-degree, 1-degree, and 1 1/2-degree increments, so dialing in alignment
is a matter of choosing the correct sleeve from a variation chart rather than turning a cam to
an arbitrary angle. The consequence for the `suspension` system is that caster and camber are
not fully independent — you pick the sleeve that best balances both against spec, which is why
the FSM lists optimum values plus tolerance ranges rather than single hard targets.
