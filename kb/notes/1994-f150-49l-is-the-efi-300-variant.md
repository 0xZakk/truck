The Ford 300 ran from **1965 to 1996**, and it changed over that span. In the **1980s Ford
added electronic fuel injection**, creating the "**EFI 300**" that powered Ford trucks
through the 1980s and 1990s, along with a move from a points distributor to electronic
ignition. The 1994 F-150 in this project is therefore the **EFI 300**, not the earlier
carbureted version.

This distinction is practical, not trivia. The EFI 300 has a **throttle body, idle air
control (IAC) valve, and EEC engine management** — which is why this truck's idle problems
involve things like the [[notes/failed-throttle-body-gasket-causes-rough-idle-vacuum-leak|throttle-body
gasket]] and [[notes/unplugging-iac-while-idling-tests-the-idle-air-circuit|the IAC]], parts a
carbureted 300 simply doesn't have. When reading older 300 advice online, always check
whether it's about the carbureted or EFI engine.

This relates to truck inventory systems `engine` and `fuel`.
