The Continuous-Loop Shoulder/Lap Safety Belt Test verifies retractor lock-up with a controlled road test
rather than a bench check. Buckle up and drive to a safe area with a dry concrete or equivalent hard surface
(never wet or gravel). Reach about 8 km/h (5 mph), warn any passenger, have everyone grasp the shoulder belt
and lean slightly forward, then make a maximum brake application — about 9.7 m/sec (32 ft/sec) deceleration —
without tire skid. The belts should lock with no webbing payout.

If both shoulder straps lock, the belts are good. If either retractor fails to lock at 5 mph, repeat the test
at a constant 24.14 km/h (15 mph); a passenger belt must be tested with an actual right-side passenger at the
15 mph step. Failure at 15 mph means the belt is faulty: remove the retractor, rework the mounting sheet
metal back to its original shape and structural integrity, reinstall, and retest. A driver alone may test the
passenger belt only at the 5 mph step by extending the right belt about 660.4 mm (26 in) without compromising
safe driving. These checks keep `interior` occupant restraints functioning.

> "The driver will make a maximum brake application of approximately 9.7 m/sec (32 ft/sec) without tire
> skid... At this instant, the front safety belts should lock up without webbing payout."
