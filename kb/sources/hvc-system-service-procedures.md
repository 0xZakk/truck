## Summary

Servicing the 1994 F-150 A/C system (inventory id `hvac`) follows a fixed sequence: recover
the refrigerant, evacuate the system to pull out air and moisture, then recharge to the
2.0 lb spec. Oil is metered back in based on how much drained from the compressor. R-134a
handling carries specific safety rules, and leaks are found with an electronic halogen
detector. This source pulls together the recovery, evacuation, charging, oil-charge, leak-
detector, and R-134 handling procedures.

## Key Points

- Recovery uses a recovery/recycling station pumped down until the system holds vacuum at
  0 psi or less for two minutes (repeat if pressure rises).
- Evacuation target: pump system to 28–29.5 in Hg at sea level; subtract 1 in Hg per 1000 ft
  of altitude. The system must be fully discharged first or the vacuum pump is damaged.
- Hold-vacuum check: low-side gauge must not rise faster than 1 in Hg every 4–5 minutes; do
  not charge until leaks are repaired.
- Charging from 14 oz cans: jumper the clutch-cycling pressure switch, draw refrigerant in on
  the low side, then run the engine in A/C/high blower to pull in the remaining charge to 2.0 lb.
- Oil charge (with external leaks): drain and measure compressor oil — <3 oz recovered add
  6 oz; 3–6 oz add the same amount; >6 oz add only 6 oz.
- R-134a is non-flammable but combustible with high concentrations of air at higher pressure;
  never heat containers above 125°F; flush skin/eyes 15 minutes on contact.
- Halogen (electronic) leak detector: move probe ~1 in/sec, positioned under the area since
  R-134a is heavier than air.

## Notable Excerpts

> "The pump should be capable of drawing the system down to 28-29.5 in Hg at sea level. For
> each 1000 foot increase in altitude, this specification should be decreased by 1 in Hg."

> "If less than 3 oz of oil are recovered from compressor, add 6 oz of new refrigeration oil
> ... If 3-6 oz of oil are recovered, add same amount ... If more than 6 oz of oil are
> recovered, add only 6 oz."

> "R-134a is combustible when mixed with high concentrations of air and higher pressures. ...
> Ensure containers are never heated above 125°F."
