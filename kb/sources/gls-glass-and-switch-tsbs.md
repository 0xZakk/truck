## Summary

Three TSBs cover non-mechanical complaints in the Windows and Glass system. TSB 94-11-18
addresses power window and door-lock rocker switches that stick in the depressed position
(causing locks to cycle continuously) due to excess plastic flash in the gray portion of the
switch bezel — the fix is to disassemble the bezel and scrape off the excess material. TSB
93-21-1 explains that iridescence or "mottling" seen in side and rear tempered glass under
certain lighting is a normal byproduct of the tempering process and is not warrantable. TSB
96-22-1 advises that windshield/interior glass fogging (film) comes from interior-trim
manufacturing chemicals, is not a health hazard, and has no field fix beyond cleaning.

Together these define which window/glass symptoms are real repairs (sticking switch) versus
expected glass characteristics that should be explained to the customer rather than replaced.

## Key Points

- Sticking switch (TSB 94-11-18): rocker switches for power windows/door locks don't return to neutral because of excess material in the gray switch bezel; locks may cycle continuously.
- Switch repair: operate the switch slowly to locate the bind, remove the bezel (one retainer screw + pull up to release the clip), unscrew and remove the switch, carefully scrape off the excess plastic, reassemble and check for binding before reinstalling.
- Iridescence/mottling (TSB 93-21-1): grayish/bluish geometric spots in side and rear TEMPERED glass are normal, caused by polarization from the tempering tension/compression — the same forces that make tempered glass strong; not reducible by scouring, intensified through polarized sunglasses.
- Windshields are ANNEALED (not tempered) and therefore do not show iridescence.
- Glass fog/film (TSB 96-22-1): caused by chemicals from interior trim manufacture; not a health hazard, no field fix; Ford Glass Cleaner E4AZ-19C507-AA is best for removal.

## Notable Excerpts

> "The rocker switches for the power windows and power door locks will not return to the neutral position... due to excess material in the gray portion of the Switch Bezel." (TSB 94-11-18)

> "Windshields, which are annealed rather than tempered, do not exhibit iridescence." (TSB 93-21-1)

> "There is no known field fix to prevent the window fogging, however, glass cleaner such as Ford Glass Cleaner (E4AZ-19C507-AA) has been found to be the best for film removal." (TSB 96-22-1)
