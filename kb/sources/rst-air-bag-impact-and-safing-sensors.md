## Summary

The SRS sensors are electrical switches that react to impacts by direction and force, discriminating
between impacts that require air bag inflation and those that do not. When an impact requiring inflation
occurs, the sensor contacts close and complete the deployment circuit. Three sensors are fitted: a primary
air bag (crash) sensor at the center of the radiator support, a primary sensor on the right frame rail, and
a safing sensor at the right cowl side trim panel. At least two sensors — one primary and the safing
sensor — must close at the same time to inflate the bag, preventing deployment from a single spurious
closure.

Sensor orientation is critical. After a crash that damaged the radiator support, frame rails, or cowls, the
sensor mounting brackets must be inspected for deformation; a damaged sensor must be replaced whether or not
the bag deployed, and the surrounding body structure must be restored to its original condition. Undamaged
sensors reset automatically after a collision and may be reused — replacement is only required when proper
alignment is impossible due to bent brackets, cut wires, or smashed connectors.

Torque specs: center sensor-to-radiator support screws 10.2-13.8 Nm (89-124 in lbs); front sensor-to-right
frame screws 10.2-13.8 Nm (89-124 in lbs); safing/front sensor-to-right cowl side panel screws 7.6-10.4 Nm
(62-89 in lbs).

## Key Points

- Sensors are direction- and force-sensitive switches that close to complete the deployment circuit.
- Three sensors: center radiator support (primary), right frame rail (primary), right cowl side panel (safing).
- Deployment requires at least one primary AND the safing sensor closed simultaneously.
- Undamaged sensors auto-reset and can be reused; replace only if physically damaged or misalignable.
- Orientation is critical; inspect brackets after structural crash damage and restore body structure.
- Center and front (frame) sensor screws: 10.2-13.8 Nm (89-124 in lbs). Safing/cowl sensor screws: 7.6-10.4 Nm (62-89 in lbs).
