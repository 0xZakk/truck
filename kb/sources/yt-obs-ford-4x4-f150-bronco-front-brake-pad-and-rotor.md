## Summary

A full step-by-step front brake pad and rotor job on an OBS (1992–1996) Ford F-150 / Bronco,
demonstrated on a 4x4 truck. The presenter covers removing the caliper (two half-inch-head
bolts on the back of the caliper, then prying the caliper off and hanging it on the radius
arm so it never dangles from the brake hose), pulling the pads and bracket, and the core
idea that whenever you replace pads you should have the rotors turned rather than just
slapping new pads on a worn surface.

The bulk of the video is the part that does **not** match a stock 2WD 1994 F-150: tearing down
the factory automatic locking front hub and the spindle/axle-nut stack to get the rotor off a
4x4. He removes the T25 hub-cap screws, pulls the locking-hub body (squeezing the internal
retaining-clip arms), then a lock ring, splined washer, flat washer, and two axle nuts using a
4WD locknut socket (OTC 4543A). He stresses the sequence for the inner ("dimpled") nut: snug it
down, back off a quarter turn, confirm the wheel still spins freely, then install the keyed
lock washer and the outer jam nut. The hub/rotor assembly and a bearing then come off, and he
notes the bearing should be cleaned and repacked with fresh grease before reassembly.

Two genuinely transferable bench tasks close it out: separating the rotor from the hub by
hammering the wheel studs out from the front (sacrificing a lug nut to protect the threads,
air-hammering the seam if it's seized), so the rotor can be turned cheaply instead of buying a
whole new assembly; and compressing the caliper piston with a C-clamp against an old pad before
the new thicker pads and turned rotor will fit. He finishes with the reassembly cautions (start
every threaded part by hand, snug the thin hub-cap screws gently in a cross pattern so they
don't snap, pump the brakes up and check fluid level before driving, then test-drive).

## Key Points

- Front caliper is held by **two bolts (1/2-in heads) on the back of the caliper**; pry the
  caliper off and **hang it from the radius arm, never from the brake hose**.
- Replacing pads is the time to **have the rotors turned** (cited as ~$10–15) rather than reuse
  a worn or scored surface.
- On a 2WD 1994 F-150 the rotor/hub comes off via the spindle nut stack, **not** the 4WD locking
  hub shown here — the locking-hub teardown (T25 cap screws, retaining clip, lock ring, OTC 4543A
  locknut socket) applies only to 4x4 trucks.
- The 4x4 inner axle nut is set "**snug, then back off a quarter turn** until the wheel spins
  freely" — the same seat-then-back-off philosophy the FSM uses for tapered front bearings.
- The dimpled inner nut + keyed lock washer + outer jam nut form an **anti-loosening stack**.
- A bearing rides on the spindle and **should be cleaned and repacked** with fresh grease (and
  leaking seals replaced) while the hub is off.
- **Separate rotor from hub** by hammering the wheel studs out from the front, with a sacrificial
  lug nut on the stud threads; re-drive studs with an air hammer and they reseat as the lug nuts
  are torqued.
- **Compress the caliper piston** with a C-clamp on an old pad so the new pads/rotor fit.
- Bleed/level: after reassembly, **pump the pedal up and check reservoir fluid level**, then test
  drive (and confirm 4WD still works, on a 4x4).

## Notable Excerpts

These are paraphrases of an auto-generated caption track, not exact quotes:

- Never let the caliper hang from the brake hose — set it on the radius arm while you work.
- Anytime you replace the pads, have the rotors turned.
- For the first (inner) axle nut on the 4x4: snug it down, then back it off a quarter turn, and
  the wheel should spin with a little free play — not so tight that the wheel won't turn.
- Sacrifice a lug nut on the stud threads before hammering the studs out, so you don't mushroom
  the ends; if a stud is seized in the rotor, air-hammer the seam to break it free.
- Always start every nut by hand a couple of turns before using any tool, so you don't
  cross-thread it; and snug the thin hub-cap screws gently in a cross pattern so the heads don't
  snap.
