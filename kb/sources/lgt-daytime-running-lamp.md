## Summary

The Daytime Running Lamp (DRL) control unit on the 1994 F-150 runs the high-beam headlamps
at reduced intensity whenever the truck is being driven without lights on. The module
activates only when three conditions are met together: the ignition switch is in RUN, the
parking brake is released, and the headlamps are turned off. Rather than running the
high-beams at full power, it outputs a pulsing voltage so the high-beam filaments glow dimly.

A diode inside the module isolates the DRL function from the low brake-fluid warning circuit,
so a low brake fluid level will not disable the DRLs. This component is part of the truck
inventory system `electrical-body`.

## Key Points

- DRL module outputs a pulsing voltage to drive the hi-beam headlamps at reduced intensity.
- It operates only with ignition in RUN, park brake released, and headlamps off.
- Releasing the parking brake is one of the enabling conditions; setting the brake turns the DRLs off.
- An internal diode keeps a low brake fluid level from disabling the DRL system.
