## Summary

The 1994 F-150's analog dash gauges — temperature, oil pressure, and fuel — share a common
"magnetic" movement design. The FSM describes the same mechanism word-for-word for the
temperature gauge and the oil pressure gauge, and the fuel gauge uses the same magnetic
indicating principle. Understanding this one movement explains how three of the truck's
gauges behave and why they are diagnosed the same way.

The movement consists of three primary coils, one of which is wound at a 90-degree angle to
the other two. Together the coils form a magnetic field whose direction varies according to
the variable resistance of the sender unit connected between two of them. A primary magnet
attached to a shaft and pointer rotates to align with that field, which sets the pointer
position. The bobbin/coil assembly is pressed into a metal housing attached to the
instrument cluster. These gauges need no adjustment, calibration, or maintenance, and the
system does NOT use an instrument voltage regulator (IVR).

## Key Points

- Three-coil magnetic movement; one coil at 90 degrees to the other two.
- Pointer position is set by a rotating primary magnet aligning to the combined field.
- Field direction (and therefore pointer position) is driven by sender resistance.
- No IVR is used — gauges read against full system voltage via the magnetic principle.
- No adjustment, calibration, or maintenance required; bobbin/coil pressed into a metal housing.
- Tester tool No. 021-00055 (or equivalent) is the FSM-specified diagnostic tool for these gauges.

### Instrument-panel mounting torque specifications

- Instrument Panel-to-Dash screws: 2.0-2.5 Nm (17-22 in-lbs)
- Selector Indicator-to-Instrument Cluster: 1.4 Nm (12 in-lbs)
- Lower Instrument Panel screw: 4-6 Nm (36-53 in-lbs)
- Instrument Panel-to-Right Side Cowl: 2.0-2.4 Nm (18-21 in-lbs)
