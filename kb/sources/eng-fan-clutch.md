## Summary

The 4.9L uses a thermal viscous (fan drive) clutch — a fluid coupling containing silicone
oil whose torque-carrying capacity sets fan speed. A coiled bi-metallic thermostatic spring
connected to an opening plate meters silicone oil into the coupling: more oil = faster fan,
less oil = slower fan, so fan speed rises with temperature and falls as the engine cools.

Diagnosis is mostly tactile. After running the engine to operating temperature (at least
five minutes immediately before testing), shut it off and feel the effort to turn the fan
by hand — considerable drag means the clutch is good; spinning freely means it has failed
and should be replaced. The clutch can also be checked on the road by disconnecting the
bi-metallic spring and rotating it 90 degrees counterclockwise, which locks the clutch into
a conventional always-on fan; if that cures the overheating, the clutch is at fault.
The FSM also describes how to interpret clutch noise, lateral fan looseness (limit 1/4 in
at the tip), and fluid leaks.

## Key Points

- Fan drive clutch is a silicone-fluid coupling; a coiled bi-metallic spring meters fluid
  so fan speed increases with temperature.
- Operational test: run to temp (5 min minimum just before testing), stop engine, feel the
  turning effort — considerable effort = good; little effort = replace.
- Drive test: disconnect bi-metallic spring and rotate 90 degrees CCW to lock the clutch as
  a conventional fan; if overheating clears, replace the clutch.
- Noise: brief noise on cold start (redistributing fluid) is normal; continuous roar or a
  fan that won't rotate by hand / feels rough = locked-up clutch, replace.
- Looseness: lateral fan-tip movement up to 1/4 in (6.5 mm) is acceptable, not cause for
  replacement.
- Leaks: small leaks are usually harmless; if free-wheeling (no drag over five hand turns),
  replace.

## Notable Excerpts

> "The fan drive clutch is a fluid coupling containing silicone oil. ... The more silicone
> oil in the coupling, the greater the fan speed, and the less silicone oil, the slower the
> fan speed."

> "If considerable effort is required, it can be assumed that the coupling is operating
> satisfactorily. If very little effort is required to turn the fan, the coupling is not
> operating properly and should be replaced."

> "This movement should not exceed 1/4 in (6.5 mm) as measured at the fan tip."
