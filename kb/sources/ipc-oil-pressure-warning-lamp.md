## Summary

The oil pressure warning lamp is a ground-switched idiot light driven by an engine-mounted
pressure switch ("engine unit"). It should illuminate when the ignition is turned ON (a
bulb prove-out) and go out once the engine builds pressure. The FSM gives a clear, ordered
test sequence that separates a bulb/circuit fault from a sender/ground fault.

If the lamp does not light with ignition ON, disconnect the wire from the engine unit and
ground it to the frame or block. If the lamp now lights, the bulb and circuit are good and
the engine unit (or its ground) is suspect; if it still does not light, replace the bulb. A
common, normal behavior is the lamp lighting or flickering at idle even with adequate
pressure — it should go out as engine speed rises. Note also that sealing compound on the
engine-unit threads will cause a poor ground.

## Key Points

- Lamp is ground-switched by an engine-mounted pressure switch (the "engine unit").
- Lamp should light with ignition ON, then go out once pressure builds (prove-out).
- No light: ground the engine-unit wire to frame/block — lights = bulb/circuit OK, engine unit suspect; still dark = replace bulb.
- Lamp staying lit when it should be out: replace the engine unit before chasing low-pressure causes.
- Flicker/glow at idle with adequate pressure is normal; should clear as RPM increases.
- Sealing compound on engine-unit threads causes a poor ground.

## Notable Excerpts

> "If it does not light, disconnect the wire from the engine unit and ground the wire to the
