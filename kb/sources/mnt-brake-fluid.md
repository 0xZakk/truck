## Summary

The 1994 F-150 uses DOT 3 hydraulic brake fluid (Ford High Performance DOT 3, C6AZ-19542-AA, or a
DOT 3 equivalent meeting Ford spec ESA-M6C25-A). Brake fluid color can legitimately vary in service
(from heat/aging, mixing brands, or dissolved spring dye) and is not by itself a fault. The real
contamination concern is hydrocarbon/mineral fluid: if power-steering or transmission fluid gets
into the system, it swells rubber parts — detectable as an obvious swelling of the master-cylinder
filler-cap gasket. If that swollen gasket is present, all brake-system rubber parts must be replaced
and all tubes flushed before returning to service.

Boiling point is the key quality metric: if brake fluid boils it forms vapor that behaves like air
in the lines, cutting the system's ability to build hydraulic pressure (it recovers once cooled).
Fluid absorbs moisture from air, lowering its boiling point, so it must be stored sealed in its
original container. This grounds `brakes` hydraulic service and fluid diagnosis.

## Key Points

- Specified fluid: DOT 3 (Ford C6AZ-19542-AA or DOT 3 equivalent to Ford spec ESA-M6C25-A).
- Fluid color varies normally; not a reliable indicator of contamination.
- Swollen master-cylinder filler-cap gasket indicates hydrocarbon/mineral-fluid contamination.
- On hydrocarbon contamination: replace all rubber parts and flush all brake tubes.
- Boiling fluid forms vapor, mimicking air in the lines and reducing braking pressure.
- Fluid absorbs moisture and must be stored sealed; moisture lowers the boiling point.

## Notable Excerpts

> "Brake fluid contaminated with a hydrocarbon/mineral based fluid (power steering or transmission fluid) can be detected by an obvious swelling of the brake master cylinder filler cap gasket. If the swollen gasket condition is present, all brake system rubber parts must be replaced."

> "If brake fluid boils in the brake system, it forms vapor which reduces the ability of the brake system to develop hydraulic line pressure."

> "Brake fluid absorbs moisture out of the air and must be stored in the original container with the cap securely fastened."
