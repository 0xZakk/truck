## Summary

A quick walkthrough of replacing the heater core on an OBS (1992–1996) Ford F-150 from
inside the cab, working at the heater case behind/under the glove box rather than removing
the dash. The narrator first lets the engine cool, then disconnects the two heater hoses at
the firewall on the engine-bay side. Inside the cab the job comes down to removing a small
electronics module that hangs in front of the case, then a plastic cover held by seven bolts,
after which the heater core can be dropped down and pulled out.

The presenter emphasizes a couple of trouble spots: two of the seven cover bolts are awkward
to reach — one is tucked behind a piece and needs an open-end wrench instead of a socket, and
another sits directly behind the module, so the module must be unclipped and set aside first.
The module is freed by pushing a small screwdriver into two clip holes to release the two
clips. He also addresses the temperature-control (blend door) cable clip that people commonly
break: instead of fighting the clip on the case, he detaches the cable end and wiggles the
hot/cold selector to gain play, making it easy to pop on and off with needle-nose pliers.

Reassembly is the reverse: feed the new core up into the case, install the new foam seal
supplied with it, then reconnect the heater hoses last (outside) so the core does not shift
while the cover goes back on. The fasteners are 8 mm / 5⁄16" head bolts. A towel is placed
under the case to catch residual coolant when the core is wiggled free.
