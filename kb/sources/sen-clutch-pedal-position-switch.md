## Summary

On the manual-transmission (M5OD-R2) 1994 F-150, the clutch pedal position switch is the
start-interlock: it prevents the engine from cranking unless the clutch is depressed. This is
the manual-transmission equivalent of the neutral safety switch on automatics — it keeps the
truck from lurching forward on a start with the clutch engaged and a gear selected.

Operation is simple and in series: depressing the clutch pedal closes the clutch pedal position
switch, which directs voltage and current to the starter relay. Until the pedal is down the
circuit is open and the starter relay never energizes, so the starter won't turn.

That series wiring makes the switch a classic intermittent no-crank suspect: a maladjusted,
worn, or unplugged clutch switch leaves the starter relay unpowered even though the battery,
relay, and starter are all fine. A common field workaround/diagnostic is to confirm the switch
closes when the pedal is fully pressed.

## Key Points

- Manual-transmission start interlock — prevents starting with the clutch engaged.
- Pressing the clutch pedal closes the switch, sending power to the starter relay.
- Wired in series with the starter relay control circuit; open switch = no crank.
- The M/T counterpart to the automatic's neutral safety switch.
- A bad or misadjusted clutch switch is a common crank-only-with-pedal / no-crank cause.
