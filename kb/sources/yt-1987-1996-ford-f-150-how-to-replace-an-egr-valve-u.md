## Summary

A short, narrated how-to on replacing the EGR valve on an OBS Ford F-150 (the demo truck
is a 1995). The host lays out the tools and parts up front, then walks through soaking the
EGR tube nut and the mounting fasteners with penetrating fluid, breaking the large tube
nut loose with a 1-1/16 inch wrench, and removing the two half-inch bolts/nuts that hold
the EGR valve and its bracket to the intake plenum. He stresses care with the tube nut:
if it shears off, you have to pull the intake manifold to replace the EGR tube, which means
fighting all the wires and vacuum hoses.

With the valve free, he covers the open tube and intake ports with a glove and paper towel
so debris and old gasket material can't fall into the engine, then wire-brushes the mating
surface and cleans it with brake cleaner. He transfers the valve to the bench, removes the
three 1/4-inch bolts holding the electrical/solenoid portion (all three snap off — he notes
the bolts are brittle and his new valve shipped with fresh ones), and fits the new gasket,
warning to keep its original orientation because the two sides differ.

Reassembly is the reverse: snug the plastic-bodied portion only barely hand-tight so it
seals without cracking, reinstall the valve to the plenum, reconnect the electrical
connector (one retaining clip had already broken — common on a 1995), and reattach the
vacuum line at the back. The whole job is presented as straightforward on this truck because
the EGR sits at the front of the engine and is easy to reach.

## Key Points

- The large EGR tube nut takes a **1-1/16 inch** wrench; soak it (and all fasteners) in
  penetrating fluid first so it doesn't shear.
- If the EGR tube nut breaks off, the fix escalates to removing the **intake manifold** to
  replace the tube.
- The EGR valve mounts to the **intake plenum** with two **1/2-inch** fasteners (one holds
  a bracket); an open-end wrench may be needed on the back nut.
- Cover the open tube and intake ports with a glove/paper towel so gasket debris can't fall
  into the engine.
- Clean the mating surface with a **wire brush, then brake cleaner**; install a **new
  gasket** in its original orientation (the two sides differ).
- The plastic portion uses three **1/4-inch** bolts that are brittle and prone to breaking;
  tighten new ones only **barely hand-tight** to avoid cracking the plastic.
- Don't forget the **electrical connector** and the **vacuum line at the back** on reassembly;
  broken connector clips are common on a 1995.
