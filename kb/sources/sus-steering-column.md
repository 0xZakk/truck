## Summary

The F-150 steering column is an energy-absorbing design built to collapse at a controlled
rate in a collision so the steering wheel is not driven rearward into the driver. The column
jacket uses a collapsing construction (slip-tube held by shearing plastic inserts/rivets, or
a slotted/corrugated mesh or bellows that shortens). The two-piece shift tube is bonded with
plastic shear pins that pare away under impact. The two-piece steering shaft telescopes
against plastic-injection resistance; its double-flatted upper section keeps the truck
steerable even after the shaft has fully collapsed. The mounting bracket uses two breakaway
capsules that release so the whole column can yield forward.

This source also covers air-bag disarming/arming, which must be done before working on the
column or wheel. This content serves the `steering` system.

## Key Points

- Column collapses at a controlled rate so the wheel does not move toward the driver.
- Jacket designs: slip-tube with shear inserts/rivets, slotted/corrugated mesh, or bellows.
- Two-piece shaft: double-flatted upper end keeps steering working even when fully collapsed.
- Mounting bracket uses two breakaway capsules that let the column yield forward.
- Air-bag DISARM: disconnect battery negative then positive, wait at least one minute for the back-up power supply to deplete, then remove the module.
- Reconnecting the battery resets PCM adaptive strategy; may need 10+ miles to relearn.

## Notable Excerpts

> "The energy absorbing function of the steering column allows the column to collapse at a
> controlled rate during a severe collision... reduces the possibility of the steering wheel
> being driven rearward towards the driver."

> "The purpose of the double flattened section is to provide continued steering action even
> though the shaft is completely collapsed."

> "Wait at least one minute for back-up power supply to deplete its stored energy."
