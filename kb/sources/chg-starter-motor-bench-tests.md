## Summary

The factory service manual gives three tests for the 1994 F-150's starter: a bench
**No-Load Test**, an in-vehicle **Load Test**, and a quick **Solenoid Test**. The No-Load
and Load tests both use the Rotunda Starting and Charging Tester (No. 078-00005) and a
carbon-pile rheostat to hold a reference voltage while the ammeter reads current draw. The
two tests bracket the starter: no-load current exposes internal faults, while load current
shows whether the starter draws normally when actually cranking.

The technique is the same for both: run the starter, record the voltmeter reading, then
adjust the rheostat (with the starter no longer drawing) until the voltmeter matches that
recorded value, and read current off the ammeter. Excess current points to mechanical drag
or shorts; the figures to compare against live in the starter electrical specifications.

## Key Points

- **No-Load Test** (bench only): uncovers open or shorted windings or a rubbing armature.
  Use cables of the same gauge as in the vehicle. Set the rheostat fully CCW (no current),
  record voltmeter reading, then reduce rheostat resistance until voltmeter matches; the
  ammeter reads no-load current draw. High current means rubbing armature, bent shaft,
  binding bearings, or shorts in armature/brushes.
- **Load Test** (in vehicle): disconnect the push-on "S" terminal at the **starter relay**
  and connect a remote starter switch from battery positive to the relay's "S" terminal.
  Transmission in NEUTRAL/PARK (auto) or clutch fully depressed (manual), crank with
  ignition OFF, record voltmeter, match with rheostat, read load current off the ammeter.
- **Solenoid Test:** check continuity between the "S" terminal and the "M" terminal, and
  between "S" and ground (frame). No continuity → check for ice, dirt, or foreign material
  preventing contact; repair or replace the solenoid.

## Notable Excerpts

> "The starter No-Load test will uncover such conditions as open or shorted windings, or
> rubbing armature. The starter can be tested, at no-load, on the test bench only."

> "Check for continuity between 'S' terminal and 'M' terminal, and between 'S' terminal and
> ground (frame). If there is no continuity, check for ice, dirt or foreign material
> preventing contact."
