## Summary

A short build-log clip from a turbocharged 4.9L (300 cu in) inline-six project truck. The
owner pulled the engine because it had been running with a **blown head gasket** and decided
that, since the head was already off, he'd take the opportunity to do head work. He briefly
considered swapping in a spare 300 that had been sitting outside, but rejected it because it
had no oil in it and he'd never heard it run — an unknown risk versus a known-good engine that
simply needed a gasket.

The most transferable part for a stock truck is the **head-gasket failure diagnosis**. He lays
the old (factory) gasket on the bench and points to the failure point where the gasket let go
between a coolant passage and a cylinder, letting coolant/water into the combustion chamber.
The affected cylinder is visibly different from the others: its combustion chamber is
discolored (rust/staining from antifreeze and water) and the piston top has been steam-cleaned
by the coolant intrusion, while the remaining cylinders look normal. The cylinder bore itself
was undamaged.

The rest of the clip is modification-specific: he **gasket-matches** the head and block — laying
the new gasket on the ports, marking the high spots, and grinding away the sharp edges so the
ports match the gasket openings. He explicitly says he is **not porting** and that these heads
don't need it; he's only knocking down sharp edges. That gasket-matching/port work is not
relevant to maintaining a stock 1994 F-150.

## Key Points

- The engine was pulled for a **blown head gasket**; it ran fine otherwise (known-good vs. an
  unknown spare that had been sitting outside with no oil).
- **Head-gasket failure signature:** a breach between a coolant passage and a cylinder lets
  coolant/water into the combustion chamber.
- **Diagnostic tells on teardown:** the affected cylinder's combustion chamber is discolored
  (rust/antifreeze staining) and its piston crown is unusually clean ("steam-cleaned"), unlike
  the other cylinders. The bore was undamaged.
- He opted for an **upgraded head gasket** on reassembly.
- Modification content (not stock-relevant): **gasket matching** — mark the gasket outline on
  the ports and grind sharp edges to match; the speaker stresses this is *not* porting.
