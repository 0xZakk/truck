## Summary

A short field tip for getting clutch pedal back immediately after replacing the
clutch slave cylinder on an OBS F-150 (the narrator's is a 1995, mechanically the
same hydraulic-clutch arrangement as the 1994). The narrator's problem is the
classic one: after the job, conventional top-down bleeding methods (pump the pedal,
crack the bleeder) gave him "zero pedal" no matter what he tried.

His fix is a reverse (back-fill) bleed. He builds a homemade tool — a large syringe
with a length of small "fish tubing" forced over the tip — fills it with DOT 3 brake
fluid, pushes the tubing onto the slave cylinder's bleed valve, opens the valve, and
forces fluid **upward** through the system until it flows out at the clutch master
cylinder reservoir. Pushing fluid from the low point up to the high point carries the
trapped air out the top rather than trying to coax it down past it, which is why the
pedal returns instantly.

This is a technique video, not a spec video: it gives no torque, capacity, or fluid
figures beyond confirming DOT 3 is the correct fluid. It is directly applicable to the
stock 1994 F-150's hydraulic clutch (`driveline`).

## Key Points

- After slave-cylinder replacement, normal pedal-pump / open-bleeder methods can leave
  you with no pedal because air stays trapped in the line.
- A **reverse / back-fill bleed** — forcing fluid in at the slave bleeder until it
  exits the master cylinder reservoir — restores pedal immediately.
- Tool is improvised: a large syringe plus small tubing ("fish hose") pressed over the
  bleed valve.
- Fluid used is **DOT 3 brake fluid** (consistent with the FSM for this hydraulic
  clutch circuit).
- The principle: pushing fluid from the system's low point up to its high point sweeps
  air out the top, instead of fighting to drive it down past the trapped bubble.
