## Summary

On the 2WD F-150 the front wheels ride on serviceable, tapered roller wheel bearings packed
in the hub behind a disc-brake rotor. The FSM gives a precise seat-then-back-off adjustment
sequence and the resulting end-play and rolling-torque targets. The procedure deliberately
over-torques the adjusting nut first to fully seat the bearings, then backs it off and
re-torques lightly, which sets a small running clearance rather than a preload.

Correct adjustment matters: too tight burns up the bearing, too loose lets the hub wobble and
chews the rotor and pads. This content is associated with the `rear-axle` inventory system
(grouped with axles, bearings and joints in the FSM).

## Key Points

- Bearings are adjusted with the wheel off the ground and the caliper kept on (do not pry the
  caliper phenolic piston); if running clearance can't be kept, remove the caliper.
- Seat the bearings: tighten the **adjusting nut to 23-34 N·m (17-25 ft·lb)** while rotating the
  rotor in the opposite direction.
- Back the nut off about **one-half turn**, then re-tighten to **2.03-2.26 N·m (18-20 in·lb)**
  while rotating the rotor.
- Resulting **end play: 0.006-0.127 mm (0.00025-0.005 in)**.
- **Torque to rotate the hub: 1.13-2.82 N·m (10-25 in·lb)**.
- Install the retainer and a **new cotter pin**, bend both ends around the retainer, reinstall
  the grease cap.
