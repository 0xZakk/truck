## Summary

The 1994 F-150 uses Rear Anti-Lock Brake System II (RABS II), which releases hydraulic pressure to the rear wheels to prevent rear-wheel lockup. It continuously monitors rear wheel speed with a RABS sensor in the rear axle housing: as the teeth on a sensor ring (mounted on the ring gear) pass the sensor pole piece, an AC voltage is induced whose frequency and amplitude are proportional to average rear wheel speed. The system acts at speeds above approximately 5 mph.

When the pedal is applied and the module senses too rapid a drop in rear wheel speed, it energizes the RABS valve to close the isolation valve, isolating the rear wheel cylinders from the master cylinder so rear pressure cannot rise. If deceleration is still too great, the module pulses the dump solenoid to bleed rear-cylinder fluid into an accumulator built into the RABS valve, dropping rear pressure and letting the wheels spin back up. The isolation and dump solenoids are pulsed together to keep the wheels rotating while maintaining high deceleration. When the pedal is released, the isolation valve de-energizes and accumulator fluid returns to the master cylinder; normal braking resumes.

RABS II consists of: the antilock electronic control module (with self-test capability), the RABS valve (a dual-solenoid electro-hydraulic valve on the left frame rail just behind the No. 1 crossmember), the RABS sensor and sensor ring in the rear axle, a yellow REAR ABS warning light, a RABS II diagnostic connector, a diode/resistor element under the power distribution box, and a RABS sensor test connector. A low master-cylinder fluid level disables the ABS and lights the indicator.

## Key Points

- Rear-axle-only ABS (RABS II) — prevents rear wheel lockup; no front-wheel ABS.
- One wheel-speed sensor at the rear axle (sensor + toothed ring on the ring gear) generates an AC signal proportional to average rear wheel speed.
- Active only above ~5 mph.
- Isolation valve closes to hold rear pressure; dump solenoid bleeds fluid into an accumulator to reduce it.
- Solenoids pulse to keep wheels rotating at high deceleration; fluid returns to master cylinder at pedal release.
- RABS valve sits on the left frame rail just behind the No. 1 crossmember.
- Electronic control module has self-test capability.
- Low brake-fluid level disables ABS and lights the (yellow) REAR ABS indicator.

## Notable Excerpts

> "When the teeth on a RABS sensor ring, mounted on the ring gear, pass the RABS sensor pole piece, an AC voltage is induced in the sensor circuit with a frequency and amplitude proportional to the average rear wheel speed."

> "If the rate of deceleration is still too great, the antilock electronic control module will energize the dump solenoid with a series of rapid pulses to bleed off rear wheel cylinder fluid into an accumulator built into the RABS valve."

> "at vehicle speeds above approximately 5 mph, the antilock electronic control module modulates hydraulic pressure to the rear wheel cylinders."
