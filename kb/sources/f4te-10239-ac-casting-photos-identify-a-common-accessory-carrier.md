## Summary

Three photographs show an actual ribbed casting with a visible Ford logo and F4TE-10239-AC identifier. It is one connected support with an arched cradle, multiple bosses and an extended narrower arm. There is no scale or dimensional drawing. The photographic evidence is useful for replacing the current separate alternator and Thermactor support approximations with a common-carrier study.

The photographer's Brazilian listing calls the part an F1000 power-steering support. That application label conflicts with US listings associating the exact casting identifier with the Ford 4.9L alternator/Thermactor bracket. The conflict is retained explicitly; the Brazilian title is not treated as authoritative US F150 interchange evidence. A separately archived exploded-engine diagram describes a common alternator/Thermactor bracket, but the available raster is too small to establish every hole function.

## Key Points

- The stamp is visually legible; its dimensions and installed orientation are not established.
- The photos show one casting rather than two independent supports.
- AB versus AC interchange and the exact 1994 mounting pattern still need Ford catalog or installed-part confirmation.
- A valid candidate needs coherent engine feet and accessory interfaces, not a union of existing arbitrary supports or coordinates selected to force a belt length.
