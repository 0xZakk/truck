## Summary

A short, cold-weather driveway demo of a cheap first fix for a 4.9L (300 cu in) that runs
poorly: hard to start, dies almost immediately after starting, and hesitates on
acceleration. The presenter frames these as common problems on these engines and traces them
to corrosion on the throttle position sensor (TPS) and idle speed control (the IAC) electrical
connectors.

The fix shown is to unplug each connector, spray contact corrosion away with brake cleaner or
electronic/contact cleaner, let it dry for a minute, and reconnect. He says cleaning the TPS
connector "usually solves almost all the hesitation issues" on acceleration. While the
cleaner dries he checks the air intake hoses for looseness, noting that on these older trucks
loose intake hoses aren't a big deal because the engine has no mass-airflow (MAF) sensor — but
the hoses should still be reseated properly.

After reassembly he restarts to confirm the repair helped. The idle air control still sticks
a little, and with about 203,000 miles on the truck he concludes it probably actually needs a
new IAC. He also mentions an unrelated brake line leak he'll address later. This is a quick,
non-authoritative driveway tip rather than a full diagnostic procedure.

## Key Points

- Symptoms: hard starting, stalling right after start, and acceleration hesitation are common
  on the 4.9L.
- First, cheap fix: clean the **TPS** and **IAC (idle speed control)** electrical connectors,
  which corrode.
- Use brake cleaner or electronic/contact cleaner; let it dry ~1 minute before reconnecting.
- Cleaning the TPS connector "usually solves almost all" hesitation-on-acceleration
  complaints, per the presenter.
- Loose intake hoses matter less on these trucks because there is **no MAF sensor**, but still
  reseat them.
- If the IAC still sticks after cleaning (here at ~203k miles), it likely needs replacement.
