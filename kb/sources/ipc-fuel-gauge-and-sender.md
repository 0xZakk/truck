## Summary

The fuel level indicating system on the 1994 F-150 is a "magnetic fuel indicator" made of
two parts: a sending unit attached to the fuel tank and a fuel gauge in the instrument
panel. The gauge creates a polarity in its wire windings; depending on the resistance seen
at the sender, that polarity controls how far the pointer rotates. The gauge operates on
battery voltage.

The sender is a variable resistor controlled by a float in the fuel tank. The FSM gives
specific resistance endpoints: when fuel is low the sender reads 22.5 ohms, and when fuel is
high it reads 145 ohms. Diagnosis is driven from troubleshooting charts: a "No fuel level
indication" pinpoint chart and combined "Fuel Gauge & Sender" symptom charts (delivered as
images in the FSM).

## Key Points

- Two components: tank-mounted sending unit + instrument-panel fuel gauge.
- Magnetic indicator: winding polarity set by sender resistance controls pointer rotation.
- Gauge operates on battery voltage.
- Sender is a float-driven variable resistor.
- Sender resistance: 22.5 ohms at LOW fuel, 145 ohms at HIGH (FULL) fuel.
- Diagnosis via "No fuel level indication" pinpoint chart and Fuel Gauge & Sender symptom charts.
