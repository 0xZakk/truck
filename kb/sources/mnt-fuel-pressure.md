## Summary

The FSM fuel-pressure test verifies that the EFI system holds correct rail pressure. With the
key off, the technician relieves system pressure at the Schrader fitting (observing the fuel
service precautions), installs a fuel pressure tester, grounds the fuel-pump lead at the
diagnostic connector with a jumper, and turns the key on with the engine off to run the pump.
The observed reading is compared to the spec for the engine being checked.

For the 4.9L the targets are 345-415 kPa (50-60 PSI) Key On / Engine Off and 310-415 kPa
(45-60 PSI) at idle. Maximum fuel pressure is obtained with the vacuum hose removed from the
fuel pressure regulator or at wide-open throttle, since manifold vacuum normally pulls rail
pressure down at idle. These values anchor `engine` fuel-delivery diagnosis: a low reading
points to the pump, filter, regulator, or a restriction; a reading that does not drop when
vacuum is applied points at the regulator.

## Key Points

- Key off; relieve pressure at the Schrader fitting before connecting the tester.
- Ground the fuel-pump lead at the diagnostic connector to run the pump Key On / Engine Off.
- Key On / Engine Off: 345-415 kPa (50-60 PSI).
- Key On / Engine Running at idle: 310-415 kPa (45-60 PSI).
- Maximum pressure is read with the regulator vacuum hose removed or at WOT.

### Fuel Filter

The fuel filter is an inline high-pressure canister with a paper element that traps dirt, water,
and contaminants before they reach the small orifices in the injector tips. Ford stresses that
clean fuel is extremely important in a fuel-injection system. The fuel filter bracket nuts torque
to 8-10 Nm (6-7 lb in). A clogged filter is a classic cause of low/sagging fuel pressure, so the
filter and the pressure test are diagnosed together.
