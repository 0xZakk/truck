## Summary

The A/C compressor on the 1994 F-150 (inventory id `hvac`) is driven by an electromagnetic
clutch. The Powertrain Control Module (PCM) monitors the A/C Clutch (ACC) apply signal and
uses it to adjust the Idle Air Control-Bypass Air (IAC-BPA) valve so that idle speed stays
correct when the compressor load comes on. The compressor clutch air gap is held to a tight
specification, and the compressor can be bench-tested off the vehicle for shaft-seal leaks.

This source consolidates the Compressor Clutch Description/Operation and Specifications pages,
plus the Compressor HVAC Testing and Inspection pages (Off Vehicle Testing and Manifold-to-
Compressor O-Rings).

## Key Points

- The PCM watches the ACC apply signal and trims the IAC-BPA valve to maintain idle speed
  when the clutch engages.
- Compressor clutch air gap specification: 0.018–0.033 in.
- Off-vehicle leak testing uses an R-134a manifold gauge set and a pressure test adapter on
  the rear head; hand-rotate the shaft ten revolutions to distribute oil before testing.
- A shaft seal leak is repaired with a new shaft seal; a center-joint (center seal) external
  leak requires a new compressor assembly.
- Manifold-to-compressor bolt torque: 13–17 ft lb. A leak there at correct torque means new
  manifold O-ring seals.
