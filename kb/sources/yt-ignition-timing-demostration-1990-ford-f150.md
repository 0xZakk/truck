## Summary

A hands-on demonstration of checking and adjusting base ignition timing on a 1990 Ford F-150
with a computer-controlled (EEC-IV) distributor — the same family of system as the 1994 4.9L
truck. The presenter first prepares the harmonic balancer by painting the timing-degree marks
so they show up brightly under the strobe: he turns the engine over by hand with a breaker bar
to bring each mark up, paints the groove and wipes off the excess. He recommends painting the
marks before the balancer is installed during an engine rebuild because it is far easier. On his
balancer the graduation marks are every 2 degrees, and he notes the small numbered marks (10, 20)
are actually harder to see than the larger unnumbered graduations.

He then connects an inductive timing light (powered from the battery, with the inductive pickup
clamped on the number-one spark plug wire in the "plug" direction). The key step for a
computer-controlled engine: unplug the SPOUT connector at the distributor before checking or
setting timing. He explicitly compares pulling SPOUT to disconnecting a vacuum-advance line on
an older distributor — it takes the computed advance out of the loop so the base timing can be
set. He loosens the distributor hold-down bolt just enough to rotate the distributor by hand
while keeping enough friction to hold position, rotates to the mark, then shuts off the engine,
tightens the bolt, and re-checks.

In this demonstration he advances the timing to roughly 14-15 degrees BTDC (rather than the
factory base setting) hoping to improve gas mileage, and observes idle rising from about 650 rpm
to around 700-750 rpm. He notes 650 rpm at 10 degrees is the factory reference. Finally he plugs
the SPOUT connector back in to return ignition-timing control to the computer. Note: the video's
14-degree target is the presenter's own mileage experiment and is NOT the factory base spec.

## Key Points

- Paint the harmonic-balancer timing marks for visibility; on this balancer graduations are every 2 degrees.
- Easiest to paint the marks before installing the balancer (e.g. during a rebuild).
- Use an inductive timing light; clamp the pickup on the **number-one** plug wire, oriented per the "plug" arrow.
- **Disconnect the SPOUT connector at the distributor before setting timing** — it removes computer-computed advance, analogous to pulling a vacuum-advance line.
- Loosen the distributor hold-down bolt only enough to rotate it by hand and still hold position, then tighten with the engine off and re-check.
- Factory reference cited: ~650 rpm idle at 10 degrees BTDC base timing.
- Re-plug the SPOUT when done so the computer resumes control of timing.
- Demonstration advances to ~14 deg for a mileage experiment — that is the presenter's choice, not the FSM base spec (10 deg BTDC).
