## Summary

Mode selection (which air doors open) on the 1994 F-150 HVAC system (inventory id `hvac`) is
done with engine vacuum, not electric motors. A vacuum reservoir stores vacuum so the air
doors hold position when manifold vacuum drops under load or acceleration, and a color-coded
nylon vacuum harness routes that vacuum from the reservoir and control assembly to the door
actuators. The reservoir can be bench-tested for leak-down.

This source consolidates the Vacuum Reservoir HVAC Description/Operation and Testing/Inspection
pages and the Vacuum Harness HVAC Description/Operation page.

## Key Points

- The vacuum reservoir temporarily stores vacuum to prevent rapid fluctuations or sudden
  drops in the signal line when the engine is under load or accelerating.
- Reservoir leak test: apply 15–20 in Hg of vacuum; loss must not exceed 0.5 in Hg in 60
  seconds, otherwise replace the reservoir.
- The vacuum harness is pre-assembled, color-coded nylon tubing (0.15 in o.d., 0.09 in i.d.)
  bonded to nylon/rubber connectors with rubber inserts for a sealed fit at component nipples.
- Damaged nylon tube sections may be replaced with 5/32 in or 7/32 in rubber hose.

## Notable Excerpts

> "The Vacuum Reservoir temporarily stores vacuum until it is needed. This temporary vacuum
> storage helps to prevent rapid fluctuations and/or sudden drops in a vacuum signal line, as
> when an engine is under load or during acceleration."

> "Apply initial vacuum of 15 to 20\". Vacuum loss should not exceed 0.5\" in 60 seconds. If
> it does, replace the reservoir."

> "The vacuum hose harness consist of colored nylon tubing (0.15\" o.d. and 0.09\" i.d.)
> bonded to nylon or rubber connectors."
