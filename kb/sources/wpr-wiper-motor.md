## Summary

The windshield wiper motor on the 1994 F-150 is a two-speed permanent-magnet DC
motor that drives the wiper linkage. The FSM provides a bench/in-vehicle current
draw test, a removal-and-installation procedure that requires removing the cowl
grille, and a Technical Service Bulletin (95-8-2) covering a revised replacement
motor and the wiring kit needed to install it.

The motor's ceramic permanent magnets are fragile: a severe physical shock can
shatter or crack them and make the motor inoperative, so the FSM opens the test
procedure with a handling caution.

## Key Points

- Wiper motor uses ceramic permanent magnets that can crack from physical shock.
- Current draw test can be done on the bench or in-vehicle with linkage disconnected.
- Current draw at both low-speed and high-speed terminals should not exceed 3.5 amps.
- Removal requires taking off wiper arms, blades, antenna, and the cowl grille.
- TSB 95-8-2: a revised service wiper motor (-17508-) and Wiper Motor Wiring Kit
  (-14A411-) are available; if the new motor is already installed, the wiring kit
  is not required.

## Notable Excerpts

> "Windshield wiper motors contain permanent magnets made of ceramic, which can
> shatter or crack if the windshield wiper motor receives a severe physical shock."

> "Connect positive (red) lead from tester... first to low-speed terminal and then
> to high-speed terminal at wiper motor. In either case, current draw should not
> exceed 3.5 amps."

> "A revised service windshield wiper motor is now available... Use the Wiper Motor
> Wiring Kit (-14A411-) to complete the installation."
