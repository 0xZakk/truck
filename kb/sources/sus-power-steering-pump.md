## Summary

The F-150 power steering pump is the Ford CII — a belt-driven slipper-type pump with an
integral fiberglass-reinforced nylon reservoir bolted to the front plate. The pressure hose
attaches with a quick-connect fitting below the filler neck; the fitting is designed to
swivel, so a hose that turns is normal and not a loose connection.

The power steering system is diagnosed with a pressure/flow analyzer (Rotunda 014-207 or
equivalent) installed at the pump outlet. The test reads backpressure, pump flow, gear
internal leakage, and control-valve/cylinder leakage, and distinguishes pump faults from
hose restrictions, a sticking gear valve, a sticking relief valve, or a binding suspension.
System pressures can exceed 1500 psi, so the gate valve must never be held closed more than
five seconds. This source covers pump operation, pump specifications, and the system pressure
test for the `steering` system.

## Key Points

- Ford CII, belt-driven slipper pump, integral nylon reservoir; pressure-hose fitting swivels by design.
- Pump model HBC-HG: relief 1300-1530 psi, min flow 1.4 GPM @ 740 psi. (Other models HBC-HN/JX/JY/KK range 1.4-1.5 GPM, relief 1350-1530 psi.)
- Analyzer connects at the pump outlet; torque the analyzer adapter connections to 15 ft lb.
- Idle backpressure above 150 psi at 170 deg = check hoses for restriction or check the gear.
- Build 740 psi (CII) / 620 psi (Saginaw) to test flow; do not hold the gate valve closed > 5 seconds.
- At the steering stops, flow should drop below 0.5 GPM and pressure should match max pump output; if not, internal leakage.
