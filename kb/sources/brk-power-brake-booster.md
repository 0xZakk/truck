## Summary

The vacuum power brake booster (2005) increases the effective foot pressure on the brake pedal using engine manifold vacuum. It is a container divided by a diaphragm into two chambers: the brake pedal-to-booster push rod enters one side, and the master cylinder push rod exits the other. If the vacuum system fails, the brakes can still be applied manually (with much higher pedal effort). The only serviceable components are the booster check valve, the adjustable push rod, and the rubber check valve grommet.

The check valve allows manifold vacuum into the booster and prevents vacuum from escaping if manifold vacuum is lost during sustained full-throttle operation, preserving a reserve of assist.

Diagnosis: visually inspect all hoses/connections (capped, secured, no holes or collapse). Operational check — with the transmission in NEUTRAL, stop the engine and apply the parking brake, pump the pedal several times to exhaust vacuum, then hold the pedal down and start the engine; if the booster works, the pedal will sink under constant foot pressure. The functional reserve check runs the engine ~10 seconds at fast idle, stops it, waits 10 minutes, then applies ~89 N (20 lb) — pedal feel should match engine-running feel. A hard pedal (no assist) points to the check valve; a spongy pedal points to air in the hydraulic system (bleed it).

## Key Points

- Boosts pedal effort via engine manifold vacuum; diaphragm splits booster into two chambers.
- Brakes still work manually if vacuum is lost (much higher effort).
- Only serviceable parts: check valve, adjustable push rod, check valve grommet.
- Check valve admits vacuum and traps it during full-throttle (low-vacuum) operation, holding a reserve.
- Working booster test: pedal sinks when engine starts while held down.
- Reserve test: after 10 s fast idle, engine off, wait 10 min, apply ~89 N (20 lb); feel should match running feel.
- Hard pedal (no assist) → replace check valve and retest; spongy pedal → bleed hydraulic system.
- Master cylinder-to-booster and booster-to-dash torque: 18-33 Nm (13-25 ft lb).

## Notable Excerpts

> "The power brake booster serves to increase, through vacuum assist, the effective foot pressure on the brake pedal."

> "If vacuum system is operating, brake pedal will tend to move downward under constant foot pressure. If no motion is felt, the power brake booster system is not functioning."

> "If brake pedal feels hard (no power assist), replace check valve and retest. If brake pedal feels spongy, bleed hydraulic system to remove air."
