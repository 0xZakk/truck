## Summary

The 1994 F-150 front and rear outboard seating positions use a continuous-loop three-point safety belt: a
combination lap-and-shoulder belt with a common sliding tongue and a single retractor. The retractor lets
webbing move freely in and out except during hard braking, hard cornering, or an impact of about 8 km/h
(5 mph) or more, when a mechanically actuated inertia sensor locks it automatically. Center positions use a
lap-only belt with no retractor that is adjusted manually by tipping the tongue and pulling the webbing; the
lap belt should sit snug and low across the hips, never on the waist. On bench seats the inboard belt and
buckle pass through the seat to a floor anchor. The continuous-loop units also include a safety belt warning
system (indicator switch, warning light, and chime) that reminds occupants to buckle up.

After any collision, all belt assemblies — retractors, buckles, front buckle support (slide bar) assemblies,
and attaching hardware — must be inspected; assemblies are replaced unless a qualified technician confirms
they are undamaged and operate properly, including belts not in use during the collision. Replacement parts
must come from the Ford Master Parts and Accessories Catalog. For child restraints, Ford recommends tethered
child safety seats go in a rear seating position with the tether strap attached to the tether anchoring
point; the right front seat is used only if it is the only position available.

The retractor lock is verified by a dynamic road test (the Continuous-Loop Shoulder/Lap Safety Belt Test):
buckle up, reach about 8 km/h (5 mph) on dry hard surface, and make a maximum brake application (about
9.7 m/sec, 32 ft/sec) without tire skid while leaning slightly forward — the belt should lock with no webbing
payout. If a retractor fails to lock at 5 mph, the test is repeated at 24.14 km/h (15 mph); failure there
means repair. A passenger belt may be tested by the driver alone only at the 5 mph step by extending the
right belt about 660.4 mm (26 in) without compromising safe driving. If webbing cannot be pulled from a
retractor at all, replace the belt.

## Key Points

- Front/rear outboard belts are continuous-loop three-point (combined lap/shoulder, common sliding tongue, one retractor).
- Retractor inertia sensor locks the belt on hard braking/cornering or impacts of ~8 km/h (5 mph) or more.
- Center belts are lap-only with no retractor, adjusted manually; keep the lap belt low on the hips.
- Continuous-loop units include a warning indicator switch, light, and chime.
- Inspect/replace all belt assemblies after any collision, including unused belts.
- Tethered child seats: rear position preferred, tether attached to the anchor; right front only if no other seat.
- Retractor lock test: 5 mph max-brake stop (no skid) on dry hard surface; retry at 15 mph; passenger-belt extension ~660.4 mm (26 in).
- All belt anchor and seat belt bolts torque to 30-40 Nm (22-30 ft lbs).

## Notable Excerpts

> "The shoulder harness retractor is designed to let the webbing freely move in or out at all times, except
> during vehicle hard braking, hard cornering, or impact of 8 km/h (5 mph) or more, when it is automatically
> locked by a mechanically actuated inertia sensor."

> "ALL BELT ASSEMBLIES SHOULD BE REPLACED UNLESS A QUALIFIED TECHNICIAN FINDS THE ASSEMBLIES SHOW NO DAMAGE
> AND OPERATE PROPERLY. BELT ASSEMBLIES NOT IN USE DURING A COLLISION SHOULD ALSO BE INSPECTED..."
