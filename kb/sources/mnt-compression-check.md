## Summary

The FSM's compression check is a relative test rather than an absolute one. Ford does not
publish a single cranking-pressure target for the 4.9L I6; instead it gives an acceptable
range based on the spread between cylinders. The procedure warms the engine, removes all
spark plugs, holds the throttle wide open, and cranks each cylinder at least five compression
strokes with an auxiliary starter switch while recording the highest reading.

Compression is considered within specification if the lowest-reading cylinder is within 25%
of the highest-reading cylinder. A spread exceeding 25% points to an improperly seated valve
or worn/broken piston rings. This makes the test a fast, grounded way to triage engine health
on the `engine` system before tearing anything down.

## Key Points

- Ford provides no fixed cranking pressure, only an acceptable range.
- Warm engine, remove all spark plugs, set throttle to wide open.
- Crank each cylinder at least five compression strokes; record the highest reading per cylinder.
- Use an auxiliary starter switch with the key off.
- Pass criterion: lowest cylinder within 25% of the highest cylinder.
- Variation over 25% indicates an unseated valve or worn/broken rings.

## Notable Excerpts

> "Indicated compression pressures are within specification if the lowest reading cylinder is within 25% of the highest reading."

> "Variations exceeding 25 percent indicate an improperly seated valve or worn/broken piston rings."
