## Summary

The 1994 F-150 windshield is bonded to the body with urethane adhesive, so replacement is a
structural repair, not a gasket swap. TSB 95-12-1 documents the Essex urethane material usage
procedure: cut the old urethane with an interior cut-out knife, remove the glass, trim the
remaining urethane bead down to a uniform ~1/16-inch (1.6 mm) layer on the pinch weld, prime
exposed bare metal, prep and prime the new glass, then lay a triangular urethane bead and set
the glass before it skins over.

The bulletin stresses two safety points: the urethane products have a limited shelf life
(decoded from an Essex batch number), and the vehicle must not be driven until the urethane
has cured, because a bonded windshield is part of the cab's structural and occupant-retention
system.

## Key Points

- The windshield is urethane-bonded; only urethane may be used to service air/water leaks on urethane-installed glass.
- Trim the residual urethane on the pinch-weld flange to a smooth, uniform ~1/16-inch (1.6 mm) layer.
- Prime exposed sheet metal with Essex Pinchweld Primer U-413 and let it dry 6-10 minutes; do not prime the existing urethane bead.
- Clean the new glass with a non-alcohol glass cleaner (e.g., Essex GC-800), apply two coats of Glass Prep U-401 (wiping off immediately), then Primer U-402 (dry 5 minutes).
- Apply a triangular urethane bead 5/8-inch (16 mm) tall by 3/8-inch (9 mm) wide around the pinch weld; set the glass before the urethane skins over.
- Open the windows before setting the glass so closing a door cannot push the glass out on the air pressure.
- Do not drive the vehicle until the urethane has cured — required for windshield retention and structural/safety compliance.
- Essex products have a limited shelf life; discard expired product (batch date decoded from the container code, e.g., JL158601AA).

## Notable Excerpts

> "Apply a triangular bead of urethane 5/8" (16 mm) by 3/8" (9 mm) wide around the perimeter of the pinch weld."

> "BEFORE INSTALLING GLASS, OPEN THE WINDOWS TO PREVENT THE GLASS FROM BEING PUSHED OUT BY AIR PRESSURE IF A DOOR IS CLOSED."

> "WHEN REPLACING URETHANE INSTALLED GLASS, THE VEHICLE SHOULD NOT BE DRIVEN UNTIL THE URETHANE HAS CURED. THIS WILL... HELP ENSURE APPROPRIATE WINDSHIELD RETENTION TO MEET SAFETY AND STRUCTURAL REQUIREMENTS."
