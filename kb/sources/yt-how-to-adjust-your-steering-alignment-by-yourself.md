## Summary

A short driveway how-to in which the host (Stevie/Steven from Minute Masters) re-centers the
steering wheel on his daily-driver OBS F-150 because the wheel sat slightly turned to the right
while the truck tracked straight. He frames it as one of the quick five-to-ten-minute tweaks he
does after work, and is also motivated by not wanting someone else who borrows the truck to drive
it off-center. The truck is an OBS F-150 with a parallelogram/drag-link steering linkage (the
video calls it "drag link steering").

He walks the linkage: the steering box moves the linkage side to side, the drag/center link
connects to the **tie rods** on each side, and each tie rod has a threaded **adjusting sleeve**.
Rotating the sleeve threads the tie rod in or out, which changes **toe** (wheels pointed in,
out, or straight). His goal is NOT to change toe — he assumes the most recent professional
alignment set toe correctly (straight or slightly toe-in) — but only to rotate the steering wheel
to line up with already-straight wheels. He notes toe-in helps the truck steer straight even with
play, while toe-out makes it darty.

To center the wheel without disturbing toe, he marks one adjusting sleeve with Sharpie at quarter
turns, labeling which direction threads the tie rod out (toward the back of the truck) versus in
(forward). Because the steering wheel is off to one side, one side's tie rod must lengthen and the
other shorten. He stresses making very small corrections — a quarter turn at a time — then test
driving, because tiny changes produce noticeable steering differences, and warns to let the
component come to a full stop before adjusting so the reading is not "all over the place."

## Key Points

- The job is **centering the steering wheel**, not setting toe — toe is assumed already correct
  from a prior alignment.
- The OBS F-150 uses a **drag-link / parallelogram linkage**: steering box → center link → tie
  rods → threaded **tie-rod adjusting sleeves** at each end.
- Rotating an adjusting sleeve threads the tie rod in/out, changing that wheel's **toe**.
- To re-center the wheel without changing total toe, **lengthen one side and shorten the other**
  by the same amount.
- **Toe-in** helps the truck track straight (tolerant of play); **toe-out** makes it darty.
- Work in **small increments (quarter turn) and test drive** between adjustments; small steering
  changes have large effects.
- Marking the sleeve with reference tick marks (Sharpie) makes the increments repeatable.
