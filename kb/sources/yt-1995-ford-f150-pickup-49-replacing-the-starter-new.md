## Summary

An independent shop owner ("Timmy" of Asheville/Online Auto Repair Videos) replaces the
starter on a customer's 4.9L (300 six) OBS F-150 with about 270,000 miles. After a long
channel-update preamble, the actual job is short and done entirely from underneath with a
small set of hand tools. He installs a **new (not rebuilt) Remy starter, part #97101**,
bought from RockAuto for roughly $50–70 with **no core charge**.

The procedure: disconnect the **negative battery cable** and tuck it away, jack the right
side up and set a jack stand (leaving the hydraulic jack in place as a backup). The starter
hangs from the bell housing by **two bolts** — he removes the **top bolt first** because it
is the hardest to reach, working a swivel socket on a long extension up over the lower
control arm and exhaust. The **bottom bolt** also retains a **ground wire** under its head.
With both bolts out the starter drops, exposing the electrical studs: a **small S (solenoid
trigger) terminal nut (10 mm)** and a larger **battery-cable nut (13 mm / ½ in)**. He stresses
photographing/noting the wire orientation before disconnecting so the wires can be routed the
same way on the new unit and kept clear of the exhaust.

Reassembly is the reverse: transfer the correct fastener sizes, set the new starter's nose in
first while clearing the **O2 sensor**, attach the heavy red cable and the S wire, start the
**bottom bolt (with the ground wire) by hand first**, then the top bolt, tighten so the starter
sits flush against the bell housing and backing plate, reconnect the battery, and crank. The
truck fires immediately. The diagnosis behind the repair: it cranked fine cold but **no-cranked
once heat-soaked** after the engine warmed up, with a known-good battery and clean connections —
a classic failing-starter symptom.

## Key Points

- Engine: **4.9L / 300 inline-six**, ~270k miles; job is identical to a 1994 F-150.
- Replacement part: **new Remy starter #97101** from RockAuto, ~$50–70, **no core charge**.
- Tools: 3/8 ratchet, **~20 in extension**, **½ in swivel (universal) socket**, stubby ratchet,
  6 in extension, **3/8-to-1/4 reducer**; the swivel/universal joint is what makes it reachable.
- Always **disconnect the negative battery cable first** and tuck it so it can't spring back.
- Two **bell-housing bolts** hold the starter; remove the **top one first** (hardest access,
  reached over the lower control arm), bottom one second.
- The **bottom mounting bolt also secures a ground wire**.
- Electrical studs: **S-terminal nut = 10 mm**, **battery-cable nut = 13 mm / ½ in**.
- **Note/photograph wire orientation** before removal and route the new wires the same way so
  they don't lean on the exhaust and short/burn.
- On reinstall, **clear the O2 sensor**, start the **bottom bolt by hand first**, and tighten so
  the starter seats flush to the bell housing.
- Symptom that prompted the swap: **cranks cold, no-crank when hot** (heat-soak) with a good
  battery — points to the starter, not the battery or cables.
