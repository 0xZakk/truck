## Summary

The cruise control system uses two independent brake-related switches to ensure it always
releases when the driver brakes. The brake on/off switch (the stop-lamp switch) sends a
signal to the PCM to disengage the torque converter clutch when the brake pedal is depressed
(the PCM may ignore this if the throttle position signal is above closed throttle). The same
switch also provides a brakes-applied signal to the speed control system (for deactivation),
the shift lock actuator, and the anti-lock brake module.

The brake pressure switch is a redundant safety device dedicated to deactivating the speed
control system. Under increased brake pedal pressure (5-10 lbs) it opens and removes power
from the servo clutch. Having two paths — the electrical brake on/off signal and the
hydraulic-pressure deactivator — means a single switch failure should not leave cruise
engaged while braking.

## Key Points

- Brake on/off switch (stop-lamp switch) feeds the PCM (torque converter clutch), speed
  control (deactivation), shift lock actuator, and the anti-lock brake module — not just the
  brake lamps.
- The PCM may ignore the brake on/off signal when the throttle position signal is above
  closed throttle.
- Brake pressure switch: redundant safety deactivator; opens at 5-10 lbs of brake pedal
  pressure and removes power from the servo clutch.
