## Summary

The 2WD 1994 F-150 uses Ford's Twin I-Beam independent front suspension. Two separate
forged I-beam axles are used, one per front wheel. The outboard end of each beam carries
the spindle and a radius arm, while the inboard (long) end reaches across the vehicle and
pivots on a frame bracket mounted on the *opposite* side. This crossed-beam layout lets
each wheel rise and fall independently while a long beam keeps camber change gentle.

The spindle is held to the beam by upper and lower ball joints (a spindle-bolt/king-pin
arrangement was used on some heavier models, with a thrust bearing carrying the load). A
spindle arm bolts to each spindle to connect the steering linkage. The front is sprung by
coil springs seated between the frame and the radius arm; the radius arm locates the beam
fore-and-aft and anchors to a frame bracket through rubber insulators.

This source consolidates the Axle Beam description plus the Radius Arm service procedure
for the front suspension (truck inventory system `suspension`).

## Key Points

- Two I-beam axles, one per front wheel; each beam pivots on a frame bracket on the opposite side.
- Outboard end of each beam holds the spindle (via ball joints) and a radius arm.
- A spindle arm on each spindle attaches the steering linkage.
- Coil spring sits between frame and radius arm; the radius arm controls fore/aft beam motion through rubber insulators.
- Radius arm removal requires removing the coil spring and the spring lower seat first.

## Notable Excerpts

> "These vehicles use two I-beam type axles, one for each front wheel. One end of each axle
> is attached to the spindle and a radius arm and the other end is attached to a frame pivot
> bracket on the opposite side of the vehicle."

> "Each spindle is held in place on the axle by ball joints or a spindle bolt which pivots in
> bushings... a spindle arm is installed on each spindle for attachment to the steering linkage."

Radius arm R&R (abbreviated): remove coil spring; remove spring lower seat from radius arm;
remove radius-arm-to-axle nut and bolt; remove rear washer, insulator and spacer from the
radius arm rear bracket; remove arm; reverse to install.
