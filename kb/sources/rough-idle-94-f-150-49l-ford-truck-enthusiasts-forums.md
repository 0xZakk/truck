## Summary

A real diagnostic thread from a 1994 F-150 4.9L owner chasing a rough idle. Tune-up parts
(plugs, wires, cap, rotor, fuel/air filter) were all under a year old and the truck
accelerated fine, so the problem was isolated to idle. The owner tested the **IAC (idle air
control)** by unplugging it while running — the idle got worse and began a rhythmic
surge-up/surge-down, confirming the IAC was doing something.

Another member pointed to a **known Ford bulletin**: the gasket between the throttle body
and the intake manifold can get sucked in and cause a vacuum leak. The owner sprayed carb
cleaner around the vacuum hoses and throttle body with no idle change (which argues against
an external vacuum leak), then pulled and cleaned the throttle body — and found the **TB
gasket had failed** and the two vacuum lines on the TB were soft and deteriorated ("like old
marshmallows"). Replacing the gasket and lines was the fix.

## Key Points

- Symptom: rough idle felt in the seat though the engine looks smooth; accelerates fine.
- Recent tune-up parts ruled out plugs/wires/cap/rotor/filters.
- IAC test: unplug it while idling — a healthy system's idle changes/worsens; here it began a
  rhythmic surge, implicating the idle-air path.
- A failed **throttle-body-to-manifold gasket** is a documented (TSB) vacuum-leak source on this engine.
- Carb-cleaner spray test around hoses/TB produced no idle change → external vacuum leak less likely; the leak was at the gasket.
- Deteriorated TB vacuum lines were replaced alongside the gasket.
