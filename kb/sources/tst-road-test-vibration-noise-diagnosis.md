## Summary

The FSM's "Component Tests and General Diagnostics" page presents a Road Test Diagnosis
table that maps customer-perceptible vibration and noise conditions to their likely
sources. It is the structured starting point for diagnosing any "the truck shakes" or
"the truck has a noise" complaint on the 1994 F-150, organizing complaints primarily by
the speed at which they occur and by how they respond to changes in drive mode
(accelerating, decelerating, steady speed, neutral coast, and standstill run-up).

A key diagnostic principle embedded in the table is the use of neutral coast as a test:
if a vibration disappears while coasting in neutral, the cause is NOT a wheel-end
(rotating) component. Likewise, an engine-rpm-related vibration that disappears in
neutral-coast but returns during a standstill engine run-up points to an engine accessory
or mount rather than the driveline. This lets a technician separate road-speed-related
faults from engine-speed-related faults before tearing anything apart.

## Key Points

- Visible shake at highway speed (72 km/h / 45 mph and up) in floor pan, seats, steering
  column, or front sheet metal → wheel/tire/axle/brake imbalance or run-out.
- If that vibration disappears in neutral coast, it is NOT caused by wheel-end components.
- Vibration felt or heard at highway speed, evident at steady speed or under light
  accel/decel → driveline or drive-axle imbalance/run-out, grounded engine mounts, or a
  bound-up exhaust system.
- Shudder or rumble on heavy accel or decel from 0–72 km/h (45 mph) → incorrect driveline
  angle(s).
- Moaning noise / belt vibration on light accel-decel between 40 and 104 km/h (25–65 mph)
  → engine or exhaust mounts bound up, powertrain components grounding, undamped powertrain
  resonance, A/C compressor pulses, or a misaligned center bearing on a two-piece driveshaft.
- Engine-rpm-related vibration/noise that disappears in neutral coast and reappears in
  standstill engine run-up → engine accessory drive or mount not properly adjusted/tightened,
  not running true, or an accessory malfunction.
- Various hums/howls/whines/knocks that vary with road speed → tire noise, wind noise,
  drive-axle gear or bearing wear/damage/misadjustment, or transmission overdrive gear noise.
- Low-frequency booming noise or ear-drum pressure at or just above idle → engine or
  exhaust mounts bound up.

## Notable Excerpts

> "NOTE: If vibration disappears while in neutral coast, then vibration is NOT caused by
> wheel-end components."

> "Shudder or rumble on heavy acceleration or deceleration, 0-72 km/h (45 mph). — Incorrect
> driveline angle(s)."

> "Engine-rpm-related vibration or noise that disappears in neutral-coast drive mode, and
> appears in standstill engine run-up at specific rpm. — Engine accessory drive or mount
> not properly adjusted or tightened, not running true, or accessory malfunction."
