## Summary

The brake on/off (stop light) switch on the 1994 F-150 is a pedal-mounted switch that does
far more than turn on the stop lamps. In addition to lighting the brake lamps, it sends a
brake-applied signal to several modules: the Powertrain Control Module (PCM) uses it to
disengage the torque converter clutch, the speed control (cruise) system uses it to
deactivate, and it also feeds the shift lock actuator and the anti-lock brake module.
Because so many systems depend on this one switch, a misadjusted or failed brake switch
can produce symptoms well beyond dark stop lamps.

The switch mounts on the brake pedal with its pushrod, nylon washers and bushings, retained
by a hairpin clip. Service is mechanical: disconnect the battery, lift the connector locking
tab, pull the hairpin retainer, and slide the switch assembly off the pedal pin. This
component is part of the truck inventory system `electrical-body`.

## Key Points

- Sends a brake-applied signal to the PCM to disengage the torque converter clutch (the PCM may ignore it if throttle position is above closed throttle).
- Also supplies the brake-applied signal to: speed control (for deactivation), the shift lock actuator, and the anti-lock brake module.
- Mounted on the brake pedal with pushrod, nylon washers and bushings, held by a hairpin retainer.
- Removal: disconnect battery ground, lift connector locking tab, remove hairpin retainer, slide switch/pushrod/washers/bushings off pedal.
- Stop light switch wires must be long enough to allow full brake-pedal travel.

## Notable Excerpts

> "The brake on/off switch sends a signal to the Powertrain Control Module (PCM) to
> disengage the torque converter clutch when the brake pedal is depressed. This signal may
> be ignored by the PCM if the throttle position signal is above closed throttle."

> "It also provides a brakes-applied signal to the speed control system (for deactivation),
> the shift lock actuator, and the anti-lock brake module."

> "NOTE: Ensure stop light switch wires are of sufficient length to allow full travel of
> the brake pedal."
