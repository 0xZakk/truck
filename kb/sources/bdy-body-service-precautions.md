## Summary

The Body and Frame Service Precautions page is the safety preamble for any cab work that
touches the battery or the supplemental restraint (air bag) system. It repeats the air bag
disarming/arming sequence and the standard battery-disconnect cautions that apply throughout
the body section. Because the driver air bag is in the steering wheel and its module has a
back-up power supply, the page insists on a one-minute wait after disconnecting the battery
before working near the air bag.

It also flags the truck-wide consequence of any battery disconnect: the Powertrain Control
Module (PCM) loses its adaptive strategy and the truck may drive abnormally until it relearns
over ~10+ miles. These precautions apply to the `body-cab` system and overlap the steering
column's air bag service.

## Key Points

- Record pre-set radio frequencies before disconnecting the battery.
- Disarm: disconnect battery negative then positive cable; wait at least one minute for the
  back-up power supply to deplete before removing the air bag module nuts and connector.
- Use the Rotunda Air Bag Simulator 105-00010 (or equivalent) connected to the harness at the
  top of the steering wheel while disarmed.
- Arm in reverse: battery off (neg then pos), wait one minute, remove the simulator, reconnect
  the module connector, install the module, reconnect the battery.
- After any battery disconnect/reconnect the PCM relearns its adaptive strategy; the truck may
  need to be driven 10+ miles before it runs normally.

## Notable Excerpts

> "Wait at least one minute for back-up power supply to deplete its stored energy."

> "After the battery has been disconnected and reconnected, abnormal drive conditions may be
> present while the Powertrain Control Module (PCM) relearns its adaptive strategy. The vehicle
> may need to be driven 10 or more miles to relearn the strategy."
