## Summary

A short clip clearing up a common misconception about the Ford 300 (4.9L) inline-six: there
is no valve-lash adjustment procedure to perform on the rocker arms. Because the 300 is a
**hydraulic-lifter pushrod engine**, the lifters take up clearance automatically, so the
rockers do not get "set" the way a solid-lifter or adjustable valvetrain would (where you'd
turn the engine and adjust each valve on the exhaust/intake events).

Instead, the procedure is simply to **torque the rocker fasteners down to about 20 ft-lb**.
The presenter notes this is the same approach used on other Ford hydraulic-lifter engines
like the 302. He says he spent time trying to find a lash-setting method before realizing it
does not apply here.

The one assembly caution he stresses: when tightening, make sure the **top of each pushrod
is seated in the pocket/cup** of the rocker arm. If a pushrod is sitting on the edge instead
of in the pocket, tightening can bend the pushrod or pop it out ("go boing"). He checks every
pushrod, more than once, before buttoning the engine back up.

## Key Points

- The 300 i6 is a **hydraulic-lifter pushrod OHV** engine — lifters self-adjust, so there is
  **no valve-lash setting** on the rockers.
- Rocker fasteners are simply **torqued to ~20 ft-lb** (the same as he describes for the 302).
- Before/while tightening, confirm each **pushrod tip is seated in the rocker pocket**, not
  riding on the edge — otherwise you can bend the pushrod or knock it loose.
- Check every pushrod, and recheck, before reassembly.
