## Summary

The Supplemental Air Bag Restraint System (SRS) on the 1994 F-150 provides increased frontal-impact
protection for front-seat occupants *in addition to* the three-point safety belt system; Ford stresses
that belts are still required for best protection. The SRS consists of an electrical system, a driver-side
air bag module, the primary (impact) air bag sensors, a safing air bag sensor, the air bag diagnostic
monitor, the air bag indicator, and a tone generator.

Electrically the SRS is powered directly from the battery and can function with the ignition switch in
ANY position — including OFF and LOCK — and even with the front seat unoccupied. The electrical system
performs three jobs: detect impacts, supply power to the igniter(s), and monitor system readiness. Its
components are the diagnostic monitor, the air bag indicator, the wiring harness with the air bag sliding
contact, the primary crash sensors and safing sensor, and the igniter inside the driver module.

Deployment is a four-step, fraction-of-a-second event: sensors detect a severe frontal impact and the
required sensor combination closes; current flows to the inflator and ignites the gas generant; rapid
burning produces nitrogen gas (and small amounts of dust, both cooled and filtered) that splits the
trim cover and inflates the bag in front of the driver; after inflation, gas vents through holes in the
bag and it deflates very rapidly.

## Key Points

- SRS supplements, does not replace, the three-point belts.
- Components: electrical system, driver-side air bag module, primary sensors, safing sensor, diagnostic monitor, air bag indicator, tone generator.
- Powered directly from the battery; works with ignition in any position (OFF/LOCK included) and seat unoccupied.
- Electrical system functions: detect impacts, power the igniter(s), monitor readiness.
- Deployment steps 1-3 occur in a fraction of a second; the bag deflates very rapidly after venting.
- Inflation gas is nitrogen, cooled and filtered during inflation.
