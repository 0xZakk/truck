## Summary

A hobbyist owner of a 1993 Ford F-150 with the 4.9L inline-six walks through a long
diagnostic ordeal to fix a truck that idled very roughly on startup, hunted up and down on
RPM, bogged and hesitated badly on throttle, and tended to stall when he lifted off the gas.
The complaint matches a classic OBS 4.9L driveability problem, and the value of the video is
the elimination process: he documents every part he replaced that did **not** fix it before
arriving at the real cause.

The parts he threw at it without success: spark plugs (all six), plug wires, distributor cap
and rotor, the ignition coil, the throttle body / throttle plate area (removed and cleaned
with carburetor cleaner), what he calls the "throttle something" sensor (TPS), the air filter,
the upstream oxygen sensor, and the fuel filter. A mechanic also disconnected a black vacuum
hose, which helped for about a day before the symptoms returned and worsened. He nearly
replaced the EGR valve but held off because it was expensive — and that turned out to be the
right call.

The actual culprit was the **MAP sensor** (manifold absolute pressure), the unit fed by the
large vacuum hose off the intake manifold and held on by a couple of small screws (a 9 mm
nut/ratchet). After swapping it, the truck started instantly, held a steady idle, and
responded cleanly to throttle. The takeaway is that a failing MAP sensor can produce a
rough-idle/no-power/hesitation complaint that perfectly mimics ignition, fuel, and air-leak
faults, so it is worth checking before spending a week replacing tune-up parts.

## Key Points

- 4.9L inline-six symptoms: rough idle at startup, RPM hunting (never holds steady),
  bad hesitation/bog on throttle, tends to stall on lift-off.
- Replacing plugs, wires, cap, rotor, and coil did **not** fix it — ruled out the ignition path.
- Cleaning the throttle body/plate with carb cleaner and cleaning the intake helped only "very slightly."
- Replacing the TPS, oxygen sensor, air filter, and fuel filter did **not** fix it.
- A mechanic pulling a vacuum hose helped for ~1 day, then symptoms returned and worsened.
- He nearly replaced the EGR valve but didn't — it was not the problem.
- The fix was the **MAP sensor**: fed by the large intake-manifold vacuum hose, held by a couple
  of small screws (a 9 mm tool), with one electrical connector.
- The fuel filter (driver's side, just ahead of the rear wheel on the frame) needed special
  curved fuel-line disconnect tools to remove.
- After the MAP sensor swap: instant start, steady idle, clean throttle response.
