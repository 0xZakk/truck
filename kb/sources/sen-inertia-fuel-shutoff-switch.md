## Summary

The inertia fuel shutoff (IFS) switch cuts power to the electric fuel pump in a collision. It is
a simple mechanical device: a steel ball held in place by a magnet. On a sharp impact the ball
breaks free of the magnet, rolls up a conical ramp, and strikes a target plate, which opens the
switch's electrical contacts and disrupts power to the fuel pump. With the pump dead the engine
quits within a few seconds.

The switch latches open after tripping and must be **manually reset** before the engine will
restart. The FSM carries an explicit safety warning: do not reset the inertia switch button if
you see or smell fuel.

For the 1994 F-150 this is a frequent real-world no-start cause — a hard pothole, a minor fender
bump, or even slamming a tailgate can trip it, and the truck cranks but won't start because the
pump never primes. Knowing the reset button exists (typically in the cab/kick-panel area) turns
a tow into a 10-second fix.

## Key Points

- Cuts power to the electric fuel pump on impact; engine dies within seconds.
- Mechanism: a magnet-held steel ball that breaks loose, rolls up a ramp, opens the contacts.
- Latches open after tripping — must be manually reset to restart.
- WARNING: do not reset if you see or smell fuel.
- Common cause of a crank-no-start where the fuel pump won't prime.

## Notable Excerpts
