## Summary

A first-person diagnosis of a recurring crank/no-start on a 1994 F-250 4.9L I6 (same EEC-IV
engine and platform-era electronics as the 1994 F-150). The owner had already chased and
replaced the usual suspects — fuel pump, spark, air/fuel — without a permanent fix. The
breakthrough came from two telltale symptoms: at key-on the fuel pump **would not cycle for
the normal couple of seconds to build pressure and then stop**; instead it just kept running.
That, combined with an intermittent check-engine light, pointed at the **PCM (powertrain
control module)** rather than any single sensor or actuator.

He describes PCM failure as a common, intermittent-symptom problem on these trucks: random
no-starts, an occasional CEL, and "lugging"/hesitation on the highway that comes and goes.
Replacing the module fixed it — the truck fired right up and ran "like a champ." He bought a
used PCM off eBay for about $150 and stresses that a replacement **must match the original by
serial/part number** for the specific truck and model year (he cites roughly the 1993–1995/96
range for this application).

The physical swap is brief: the module is held by a **10 mm bolt**, sits behind a rubber
weatherproof surround/boot, and you must loosen the fender liner for clearance to pull it out.
Opening the old unit revealed mild burn damage on a couple of board components (he calls them
"diodes"), which he believes is consistent with a failing module — and speculates the bad PCM
may have contributed to his earlier fuel pump burnout by keeping the pump running continuously.

## Key Points

- Two key clues pointed to the PCM: **fuel pump runs continuously at key-on (never cycles
  off after the prime)** and an **intermittent check-engine light**.
- Symptom pattern was intermittent: random no-start, occasional CEL, and hesitation/"lugging"
  while driving that came and went — classic of a marginal control module.
- PCM failure is described as a **common failure point** on these 4.9L OBS trucks.
- Replacement PCM was a used eBay unit (~$150); a replacement **must match the original
  serial/part number** and model year (he cites ~1993–1995/96 for this app).
- Physical R&R: one **10 mm bolt**, a rubber weatherproofing surround, and **loosen the
  fender liner** for clearance to remove it.
- A continuously-running pump (no cycle-off) can overheat and may have caused his earlier
  fuel-pump failure.
