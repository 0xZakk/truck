## Summary

The 1994 F-150 uses a pedal-actuated dual (split) hydraulic brake system as standard equipment. The system consists of a power brake booster, a brake master cylinder (2140), brake pressure control valves, brake tubes and hoses, and anti-lock brake system (ABS) components. Front brakes are self-adjusting disc brakes; rear brakes are drum brakes with automatic brake-shoe adjusters. The master cylinder is assisted by a vacuum power brake booster (2005).

The hydraulic system is filled with DOT 3 brake fluid, whose color and properties must conform to Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 116. Brake fluid is hygroscopic — it absorbs moisture from the air, which lowers its boiling point over time. If brake fluid boils in the system it forms vapor that behaves like trapped air, reducing the system's ability to develop hydraulic line pressure, with normal operation returning only after the brakes cool and the vapor condenses.

Cleaning and inspection of hydraulic parts is done with clean isopropyl alcohol (or a Rotunda Brake Parts Washer). If dirt is found anywhere in the hydraulic system, the entire system must be flushed with clean isopropyl alcohol. Cylinder bores are checked for rust, scoring, and damage, and bleeder screw passages are verified clean and open.

## Key Points

- Dual (split) hydraulic system: front circuit and rear circuit are independent.
- Front: self-adjusting disc brakes. Rear: drum brakes with automatic shoe adjusters.
- Fluid: DOT 3, conforming to FMVSS 116; Ford Heavy-Duty Brake Fluid C6AZ-19542-AA (ESA-M6C25-A).
- Brake fluid absorbs moisture; store sealed; moisture lowers boiling point.
- Boiling points (dry/wet): DOT 3 401/284°F; DOT 5 500/356°F; Ford DOT 3 550/550°F.
- Brake hose/line fitting torques by thread: 3/8-24 and 7/16-24 → 13-20 Nm (10-15 ft lb); 1/2-20 and 9/16-18 → 20-28 Nm (15-21 ft lb).
- Master cylinder-to-booster nut and booster-to-firewall: 18-34 Nm (13-25 ft lb).
- Clean hydraulic parts only in isopropyl alcohol; flush whole system if dirt found.

## Notable Excerpts

> "If brake fluid boils in the brake system, it forms vapor which reduces the ability of the brake system to develop hydraulic line pressure... it reverts to normal operation when the brakes cool and the vapor condenses."

> "Brake fluid absorbs moisture out of the air and must be stored in the original container with the cap securely fastened. If not, its moisture content will increase and the boiling point will drop."

> "The vehicles are equipped with a pedal actuated dual brake system."
