## Summary

On the Twin I-Beam front end the spindle is held to the axle beam by an upper and a lower
ball joint. Both are checked the same way: with the front wheel bearings correctly adjusted
and the wheel raised, the tech rocks the tire in and out and watches for play at the joint.
The FSM gives a single, simple wear limit — 1/32 inch of movement — that condemns either
joint.

The lower joint is checked by grasping the lower edge of the tire and watching the lower
spindle arm and the lower portion of the axle jaw; the upper joint is checked by grasping
the upper edge of the tire. Because a loose wheel bearing mimics ball-joint play, the
procedure insists the bearings be adjusted first. This is core front-end diagnosis for the
`suspension` system.

## Key Points

- Adjust front wheel bearings before checking ball joints (loose bearings fake ball-joint play).
- Lower joint: grasp lower edge of tire, move wheel in/out, watch lower spindle arm and lower axle jaw.
- Upper joint: grasp upper edge of tire, move wheel in/out.
- Wear limit for BOTH joints: more than 1/32 inch of movement = replace that joint.

## Notable Excerpts

> "Check lower ball joint by grasping lower edge of tire and moving wheel in and out while
> observing lower spindle arm and lower portion of axle jaw. If movement exceeds 1/32 inch...
> the lower ball joint must be replaced."

> "Check upper ball joint by grasping upper edge of tire and moving wheel in and out. If
> movement exceeds 1/32 inch, the upper ball joint must be replaced."
