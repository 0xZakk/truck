# Reference photo shot list — the ground truth for the 3D model

Goal: enough coverage that every visible part, panel line, and routing can be
modeled and verified against **your actual truck**, not a generic '94 F-150.

Drop everything into `reference/photos/` (any filenames; I'll catalog them).
Phone camera is fine. Favor overcast daylight or open shade — avoid harsh sun
and deep shadows. Get close enough that the subject fills the frame, and when
in doubt, shoot more — extra angles are never wasted.

## A. Exterior walkaround (~24 shots, 10 min)
- Full truck from 12 positions around it (every ~30°), camera at chest height,
  whole truck in frame. These drive body proportions and panel lines.
- Straight-on: front, rear, both sides, and a high 3/4 front if you can hold
  the phone overhead or stand on something.
- Straight-down-the-side shot from each end (to capture body side crown).

## B. Panel & trim closeups (~20 shots, 10 min)
- Each wheel opening (all 4), square-on — arch shape vs. tire.
- Grille + headlights + turn signals, straight on and 3/4.
- Both bumpers: face, ends (the wrap-around), and from below.
- Door handles, mirrors, B-pillar area, drip rail, badges, side molding.
- Tailgate (closed + open), taillights, rear bumper step.
- Fuel filler, antenna base, wipers/cowl vents, door cut lines at A/B pillars.
- One wheel dead-square-on (rim detail), plus the spare under the bed.

## C. Engine bay (~20 shots, 15 min)
- Hood open: one wide shot from each side and from the front (3 total).
- Driver side: exhaust manifold, steering box area, brake booster + master.
- Passenger side: intake side, fuel lines, heater hoses, A/C lines.
- Front: radiator, fan, belts/pulleys (square-on so I can read the belt routing).
- Closeups of: air cleaner, valve cover, distributor + plug wire routing,
  alternator, water pump area, battery + cable routing, starter relay on the
  fender, wiring harness runs along the fenders and firewall.
- The firewall, straight on if possible.

## D. Underbody (~15 shots, 15 min — only as far as you can safely reach)
No need to crawl fully under if it's not safe — shoot from the sides at ground
level with the phone tilted in.
- Front: I-beam suspension, springs, shocks, steering linkage, sway bar.
- Mid: transmission, driveshaft (both u-joint ends + center bearing), frame rails,
  fuel tank + straps, exhaust routing past the cab.
- Rear: leaf springs + shackles, axle + diff cover, brake lines along the axle,
  spare tire carrier, hitch.
- Starter, oil pan, clutch slave/linkage area from below if reachable.

## E. Interior (~15 shots, 10 min)
- Dash: straight on (driver seat), full width; gauge cluster closeup; HVAC
  controls; radio area; steering wheel + column stalks.
- Pedals + under-dash (fuse box, wiring under the column).
- Bench/seats square-on, door panels (both sides), headliner, dome light.
- SuperCab rear area: jump seats / rear trim, the area behind the seats.
- Shifter + floor area, kick panels, sill plates with the door open.

## F. Anything else
- VIN plate / door jamb certification label (build data — confirms axle, paint
  code, GVWR; I'll decode it).
- Any part you ever have off the truck: lay it on the ground next to a tape
  measure and shoot it from 4–6 angles. That's a free 3D-reference for that part.

**Total: ~90–100 photos, roughly an hour.** Sections A–C matter most; D and E
can be a second session.
